## Question 3. Context-sensitive "B" pads

### Takeaway
A pad is a marked spot where pressing B makes Conker do whatever that spot is for: take a hangover cure, become an anvil, pull out a shotgun or a slingshot. It replaces a long learned move list with one button, lets each scene have its own bespoke action and joke, and tells the player what the scene wants. Critics split: IGN said the pads keep the game fresh and cut the collecting, Edge said they turn it into a string of barely connected minigames. I found no statement from Chris Seavor or Rare saying in so many words why they made them.

### Cited Findings
- **READ** What they are. Quote: "Some pads can turn Conker into an anvil to slam into the ground". Others pull out "his shotgun, blow torch, throwing knives, and slingshot". https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conker%27s_Bad_Fur_Day
- **READ** The first one is the tutorial and a gag at once. Quote: "Conker drinks some Alka-Seltzer to relieve his hangover". https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conker%27s_Bad_Fur_Day
- **READ** They are signposts. Quote: "They also serve to inform players of what needs to be done next." https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conker%27s_Bad_Fur_Day
- **READ** They go with a deliberately small move set. Quote: "Conker's abilities are simpler" (compared with Banjo-Kazooie and Donkey Kong 64). https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conker%27s_Bad_Fur_Day
- **READ** The nearest thing to a stated reason: Seavor wanted every action to have a story motive. Quote: "Just doing a 'thing' like hitting something with a brick is far more engaging if there's a motivation behind it". https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conker%27s_Bad_Fur_Day
- **READ** The mission formula the pads serve. Quote: "This set the formula for later missions: an introduction, interaction of the mission". The third part is an "extreme punchline" cutscene as the reward. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conker%27s_Bad_Fur_Day
- **READ** Levels got easier to build when the joke came first. Quote: "being less focused on gameplay and more on the comedic premises made the levels come together easier". https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conker%27s_Bad_Fur_Day
- **READ** A cut mission shows a pad used as the trigger of a set piece. Quote: "then going to a context-sensitive area to shoot the fish". https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conker%27s_Bad_Fur_Day
- **READ** For: IGN's Matt Casamassina. Quote: "help keep the action shifting, refreshing, and always exciting". He also "credited Rare for reducing the number of items to collect". https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conker%27s_Bad_Fur_Day
- **READ** For: GameRevolution's Johnny Liu. Quote: "a staccato flow between gameplay and cutscenes". The fun, he said, was in working out what to do next. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conker%27s_Bad_Fur_Day
- **READ** For: GamePro called the varied missions "sublime satirical genius", which it felt made up "for the linear gameplay". https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conker%27s_Bad_Fur_Day
- **READ** Against: Edge. Quote: "little more than a procession of barely connected and puerile minigames". https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conker%27s_Bad_Fur_Day
- **READ** Against: Next Generation's Chris Slate. Quote: "many puzzles lack logical coherence and depended too much on". The sentence ends "trial and error"; he was also bothered by the lack of direction on where to go. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conker%27s_Bad_Fur_Day
- **READ** Rare had used pads before. In Banjo-Kazooie, flying and the high spring jump work only on marked pads. Quote: "these moves can only be activated by standing on pads signifying them". https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banjo-Kazooie_(video_game)
- **READ** The 2005 remake plays on the player's memory of the tutorial: the gargoyle no longer falls to the frying pan, so "Conker pulls out a baseball bat". (Wikipedia does not say whether this happens on a pad.) https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conker:_Live_%26_Reloaded
- **SEARCH SUMMARY** (Giant Bomb; not read) A light bulb appears over Conker's head when he stands in a context zone; away from pads, B is the frying pan. Giant Bomb argues the mechanic kept the game on story and puzzles and away from collecting. https://giantbomb.com/wiki/Games/Conkers_Bad_Fur_Day
- **SEARCH SUMMARY** (Giant Bomb "Birdy", Nintendo Life review, GamesFirst review; not read) Birdy the scarecrow explains the pads in the first chapter, and one review compares the exchange to a "Who's on First" routine. https://giantbomb.com/wiki/Characters/Birdy and https://gamesfirst.com/articles/2001/conkers-bad-fur-day-n64/
- **SEARCH SUMMARY** (GameCritics review by Brad Gallaway; not read) He called the context areas a good idea that he would like to see taken further, and praised the sharp cut in collecting compared with Banjo-Tooie and Donkey Kong 64. https://gamecritics.com/brad-gallaway/334/

### Inferences (mine)
- The pads look like Banjo-Kazooie's flight and spring pads generalised: instead of "this spot allows move X", it is "this spot allows whatever the scene needs". That lets a writer-led team add a one-off action without touching the control scheme.
- They shape level design in a specific way: each room becomes "find the pad, read the situation, do the bit", with the cutscene punchline as payment. That is why reviewers describe the game as scenarios, and why Edge could call it minigames.
- The honest cost: a pad action has no skill curve. The fun is in the surprise and the staging, so it wears out on replay, and it cannot carry combat or platforming.

### Gaps
- No direct quote from Seavor, Mayles or anyone at Rare on why the pads exist or who proposed them. Several searches for one came back empty.
- How many pads there are, and the full list of what they do: not found.
- The original Edge, IGN and Next Generation reviews were not read; only Wikipedia's quotations of them.

## Question 4. Structure: hub, chapters, money, set pieces, bosses, pacing, complaints

### Takeaway
The game is a linear run of comic scenarios hung on a hub: you help a character, get a cutscene punchline and a wad of cash, and cash opens the next area. Money is the only thing collected, and even it stops mattering before the last three chapters. The scenarios are increasingly film parodies, and the late game turns into a shooter. Reviewers praised the variety and pacing and complained about the camera, vague direction, trial and error, and (some) the length.

### Cited Findings

Hub, chapters, money
- **READ** The loop. Quote: "The result is always a cash reward, which aids access to other areas in the overworld." https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conker%27s_Bad_Fur_Day
- **READ** Levels are bounded playgrounds with jobs in them. Quote: "Each level is an enclosed area in which the player can freely explore to find tasks to do." https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conker%27s_Bad_Fur_Day
- **READ** Why the game is cut into chapters: save memory. Quote: "In order to fit three save files in four kilobytes of SRAM space, the game was broken up into lengthy chapters." https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conker%27s_Bad_Fur_Day
- **READ** Money has prices attached to people: $1,000 to the weasel guards for Uga Buga, $2,110 to Mr. Barrel for Spooky. Quote: "$2,110 is needed to pay Mr. Barrel". https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conker%27s_Bad_Fur_Day
- **READ** Greed is the story as well as the system. Quote: "becomes sidetracked from his goal in pursuit of monetary rewards". https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conker%27s_Bad_Fur_Day
- **READ** Health is chocolate, and the game jokes about why it floats. Quote: "makes fun of floating collect-able items in other games". https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conker%27s_Bad_Fur_Day
- **READ** Lives are explained by a character (Gregg the Grim Reaper), met on the first death. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conker%27s_Bad_Fur_Day
- **SEARCH SUMMARY** (conker.fandom.com chapter pages, TV Tropes; not read) Hungover is the tutorial chapter and Windy the second. Spooky is given as the seventh chapter, between Uga Buga and It's War. Spooky and It's War contain no money. Chapters can be replayed from the main menu once unlocked (Giant Bomb). One summary calls Barn Boys the "fourth" chapter, which does not fit the other numbers; see Gaps. https://conker.fandom.com/wiki/Spooky , https://conker.fandom.com/wiki/Barn_Boys , https://giantbomb.com/wiki/Games/Conkers_Bad_Fur_Day
- **FROM GENERAL KNOWLEDGE, UNVERIFIED** The nine chapters in order, as I remember them: Hungover, Windy, Barn Boys, Bats Tower, Sloprano, Uga Buga, Spooky, It's War, Heist. Wikipedia names all of these except Hungover but does not give an order.

Set pieces and parodies
- **READ** Parody became the method. Quote: "The parodies helped the developers come up with ideas for music, sound, design, and gameplay." https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conker%27s_Bad_Fur_Day
- **READ** It began by accident, with a Terminator reference "in the barnyard scene". Films named: A Clockwork Orange (the intro), The Matrix (a lobby chase scene), Saving Private Ryan (the start of It's War), Jaws (Brute), Aliens (the final boss), Dracula, Star Wars, Apocalypse Now. Quote: "The final boss fight references Aliens (1986)". https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conker%27s_Bad_Fur_Day
- **READ** The genre shifts late. Quote: "its later sections featuring elements of shooters". https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conker%27s_Bad_Fur_Day
- **READ** The game mocks its own structure. Quote: "Conker makes fun of how menial his missions are". The villain wants a squirrel as a table leg, a jab at thin game plots. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conker%27s_Bad_Fur_Day
- **READ** About two hours of the game is cutscene. Quote: "The length of all cutscenes combined total around two hours." https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conker%27s_Bad_Fur_Day
- **READ** (Game Developer blog; opinion) The parodies did not shut out a player who had not seen the films. Quote: "I didn't feel at all removed from the game or detached from all of the references". https://www.gamedeveloper.com/business/conker-s-bad-fur-day-as-a-case-study-for-cult-hits-
- **SEARCH SUMMARY** (TV Tropes "Trivia", quoting a Play Magazine interview; not read) Seavor is quoted as saying that if he did it again, the whole game would be a movie parody. https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/Trivia/ConkersBadFurDay

Bosses
- **READ** Quote: "The bosses also take four hits to kill, a twist on the typical three in other video games." https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conker%27s_Bad_Fur_Day
- **READ** The Great Mighty Poo sings opera during his fight, and beating him opens a pipe onward. The final boss, Heinrich, is fought in "a robotic suit" and then ended by a fake crash of the game. Quote: "the game suddenly freezes". https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conker%27s_Bad_Fur_Day
- **SEARCH SUMMARY** (WhatCulture, Giant Bomb; not read) Other bosses named: the Haybot, the Big Big Guy, Buga the Knut. https://whatculture.com/gaming/10-most-underrated-nintendo-64-boss-battles

Pacing, difficulty, camera
- **READ** Praise for pacing (IGN). Quote: "remarkably deep, well-paced level design, tightly knitted control mechanics". https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conker%27s_Bad_Fur_Day
- **READ** It is one road. Quote: "there's only one path to traverse the game's big world". https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conker%27s_Bad_Fur_Day
- **READ** Sources disagree on length. Official Nintendo Magazine: "a monster sized game". Wikipedia's summary of other critics lists "short length and linear nature" among the complaints. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conker%27s_Bad_Fur_Day
- **READ** The camera was fixed and far away on purpose. Quote: "To make the game look cinematic, Rare went with having a fixed camera that was very zoomed out." https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conker%27s_Bad_Fur_Day
- **READ** Camera complaints. GameSpot: it "can get caught on objects or refuse to obey commands". N64 Magazine: players cannot properly judge their position. San Francisco Chronicle: the character is "difficult to control, especially when required to jump onto small areas". https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conker%27s_Bad_Fur_Day
- **READ** One section is notorious. Quote: "named the water tube the 64th most challenging level of all video games". https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conker%27s_Bad_Fur_Day
- **READ** Shock humour was predicted to fade: Slate "felt the shock effect would go away within an hour". https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conker%27s_Bad_Fur_Day
- **READ** The remake fixed what was complained about. Quote: "the camera control was refined and improved with a zoom function, and an auto-targeting system was added". https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conker%27s_Bad_Fur_Day
- **SEARCH SUMMARY** (user reviews hosted on Metacritic and small sites; not read) Later players complain of difficulty spikes, long stretches without checkpoints (Spooky is singled out), and sudden genre changes. https://www.metacritic.com/game/1300000705/

Multiplayer (for completeness)
- **READ** Seven modes for up to four players: "Beach, Raptor, Heist, War, Tank, Race and Deathmatch". https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conker%27s_Bad_Fur_Day

### Inferences (mine)
- "Money as the only collectible" is really "no collectibles": cash is a score for finished scenes and a key for the next door, and the designers stop handing it out once the last door is paid for. The reward the player actually works for is the punchline cutscene.
- The structure is closer to a sketch show than to Mario 64: a hub that strings sketches together. That explains both the praise (variety, pacing) and the two standing complaints (where do I go, and why did that puzzle work).
- The camera complaint is a direct result of a choice made for looks. A far, fixed camera flatters a scene and hurts precise jumps.

### Gaps
- The exact chapter order and each area's cash price: not confirmed by anything I read; one fan-wiki summary is inconsistent about Barn Boys' number.
- The total cash in the game: not found.
- A full boss list with how each is fought: not found beyond the names above.
- Play time: sources disagree and none gives hours.

## Question 5. Movement and controls: Conker's move set, feel, camera

### Takeaway
Conker does little: run, jump, a higher jump from a crouch, a short tail-spin hover, a frying-pan swipe, swim, climb, push. Everything else comes from pads. Reviewers at the time called the controls tight; the trouble was the camera and jumps onto small targets, and one reviewer found the single-player handling too slow for the multiplayer shooter modes.

### Cited Findings
- **READ** The base set. Quote: "The player can run, jump, and smack enemies with a frying pan". https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conker%27s_Bad_Fur_Day
- **READ** Quote: "Conker can also swim underwater for a limited period of time, climb ladders or ropes, and push objects." https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conker%27s_Bad_Fur_Day
- **READ** The frying pan was chosen for its sound. Quote: "because it justified the use of a comic sound effect of a metal object hitting something". It replaced a baseball bat. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conker%27s_Bad_Fur_Day
- **READ** He walks upright; the cancelled version did not. Quote: "moved on four legs in". https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conker%27s_Bad_Fur_Day
- **READ** His tail took a lot of animation work and was simplified by thinking of it as a "bag of air". https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conker%27s_Bad_Fur_Day
- **READ** The team studied Mario 64 for the camera. Quote: "The developers heavily analysed Super Mario 64". https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conker%27s_Bad_Fur_Day
- **READ** Handling in multiplayer. Liu said the single-player responsiveness and control setup "were not suitable for the fast pace of the multi-player battles". https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conker%27s_Bad_Fur_Day
- **READ** (Game Developer blog; opinion) Quote: "It had nice and tight controls". https://www.gamedeveloper.com/business/conker-s-bad-fur-day-as-a-case-study-for-cult-hits-
- **READ** The remake changed aiming. GameZone praised "a new targeting system that takes advantage of strafing". https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conker:_Live_%26_Reloaded
- **SEARCH SUMMARY** (StrategyWiki "Controls", Nintendo Life review, conker.fandom.com; not read) N64 mapping: stick to move, A to jump, Z to crouch (move while crouched to crawl), Z plus A for a high jump, B to attack (the frying pan unless the situation changes it), C buttons for the camera, R to aim, and Z as the trigger when a gun is out. Conker can spin his tail like a helicopter for a few seconds to hover (walkthroughs call it the "Helicoptery Tail"). Nintendo Life's review is summarised as saying the beauty of the controls lies in their simplicity. https://strategywiki.org/wiki/Conker's_Bad_Fur_Day/Controls and https://www.nintendolife.com/reviews/2009/04/conkers_bad_fur_day_retro
- **SEARCH SUMMARY** (a later small-site review; not read) One reviewer says the moving and jumping are less refined than in Rare's other N64 platformers. https://infinityretro.com/conkers-bad-fur-day-review

### Inferences (mine)
- The move set is small by design so that the scene, not the player's hands, carries the variety. This is the opposite bet to RatQuest, whose appeal is a rich move set (double jump, wall kick, shield ride, lock-on fighting).
- The hover is the one "feel" move: a short tail spin that forgives a misjudged jump. With a far fixed camera, that forgiveness was needed.

### Gaps
- No numbers for run speed, jump height or hover time; nothing like the decompiled data available for Mario 64.
- The manual's own wording of the moves could not be read.
- How the camera is controlled in detail (which sections are fixed, how the C buttons behave) is known only from summaries.

## Ideas this suggests for RatQuest
All of these are my suggestions, each tied to a finding above.

- **Use "pads" in the Club, not in the levels.** Conker's pads suit one-off comic actions and cannot carry skill. RatQuest's levels already have a real move set. In the hub, though, a few marked spots where one button does the fitting thing (sit at a table, take a drink, throw a dart, bang the shield on the bar for service) would make the knight a participant in the room at very low cost, and each can end in a small punchline.
- **Let a pad be a signpost.** Pads "inform players of what needs to be done next". A glowing stool next to the regular who has a job for you is a wordless quest marker.
- **Put prices on people, not on doors.** Mr. Barrel and the weasel guards take the cash. In the Club, the route to a new world can be a regular who must be paid, persuaded or beaten at something, which also gives that regular a reason to be standing there.
- **Adopt the three-beat scene: set-up, play, punchline.** Seavor's formula is a cheap discipline for any hub errand or level task: a character states a problem, the player does one thing, a short staged gag pays it off.
- **Start from the comic premise when a level will not come together.** The team found that levels "come together easier" when the joke came first. For a stuck sewer level, ask what the funny situation is before asking what the mechanic is.
- **Keep one currency and let it run out of use on purpose.** Conker stops giving cash once the last gate is paid. If RatQuest's coins open worlds, plan the last purchase and what coins mean after it (Club drinks, jokes, cosmetics for the bar).
- **Explain game rules with characters.** Gregg explains lives; Birdy sells the manual. Dizzy Dave or a regular can be the in-world reason for checkpoints, continues or the map.
- **Do not copy the camera.** A far fixed camera was chosen to look cinematic and became the most repeated complaint, especially for small landings. For RatQuest's platforming keep the player-controlled camera; reserve fixed, composed angles for the Club, where nothing needs a precise jump.
- **If a far camera is ever used, add a forgiving move.** Conker's tail hover covers bad depth judgement. RatQuest's double jump already does this job; do not design hub jumps that need more precision than it forgives.
- **Beware the "minigame procession".** Edge's criticism is the risk of any scene-by-scene design. Vary the Club errands, but have most of them use the knight's normal moves (a shield-ride delivery, a wall-kick shortcut to the rafters) so the hub exercises the core game.
- **Signal the next step.** The standing complaint was not knowing where to go. With a hub full of talkers, make sure exactly one of them is visibly "the one with the job" at any time.
- **Four hits, not three.** A small subversion of boss convention was part of the game's humour. RatQuest's bosses, which rely on tells, could each break one genre habit in the same spirit.

### What Conker was turning away from: Rare's own collecting (Donkey Kong 64, 1999)
- **READ** The game Rare shipped sixteen months before Conker was built on MORE things to pick up, by order from the top. Quote: "The strong emphasis on collectibles was a design choice made at the request of Rare co-founder" Tim Stamper; the director recalls being told "No, more things". https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donkey_Kong_64
- **READ** Reviewers at the time already objected. Quote: "criticized its camera and emphasis on item collection and backtracking". One called it "an interactive [[egg hunt]]" (brackets are Wikipedia's link marks). https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donkey_Kong_64
- **READ** The verdict stuck to the studio. Quote: "Rare's 3D platformers became notorious for their emphasis on collecting items". A later developer "blamed" the game for the genre's decline. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donkey_Kong_64
- **READ** The scale: "200 goals in total", five characters each with their own colour of banana. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donkey_Kong_64
- **READ** (re-check, for the contrast) IGN's reviewer "credited Rare for reducing the number of items to collect" in Conker. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conker%27s_Bad_Fur_Day

### When a one-off, context-sensitive action works (general design writing, read in full)
- **READ** (Game Developer blog, Joshua Hallaran, 5 July 2017; about a 2D RPG) The problem stated. Quote: "you generally don't want to tutorialise and spend time on something that the player will rarely encounter, but you also need to clearly communicate what the player needs to do". https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/organically-teaching-and-implementing-context-sensitive-mechanics
- **READ** (same) His three rules are a visible goal, the buttons the player already knows ("Same Methods, New Interactions"), and speed ("Keep it Quick"). On how often: "using a context-sensitive interaction once is too rare", and "this type of mechanic should remain a novelty, instead of overstaying its welcome". https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/organically-teaching-and-implementing-context-sensitive-mechanics
- **READ** (Game Developer blog, Evan Hemsley, 4 November 2015; about a first-person platformer) A button that does nothing outside its context confuses. Quote: "Inputs should never be contextually useless." His example: a grapple that would not throw without a target, which players did not realise existed; and "inputs should always have immediate and clear feedback (even if that feedback is negative!)". https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/don-t-surprise-me-or-preventing-input-related-frustration
- **READ** (CritPoints, Celia Wagar, 19 March 2015; opinion, about quick-time events) What makes a context action tolerable to a critic who dislikes most of them. Quote: "this is a context sensitive action and you're not locked out of your other options to do that"; and of the good ones, "They all consistently use the same inputs." https://critpoints.net/2015/03/19/making-good-qtes/

### Inferences (mine, second pass)
- Conker's pads meet Hallaran's and Hemsley's tests better than their reputation suggests: the pad is the visible goal, the button is always B, B always does something (the frying pan) away from a pad, and each bit is quick. Where they fail Hallaran's "once is too rare" is exactly where Edge's "minigames" complaint lands: many pad actions are used once and never again.
- For a game with a real move set, the safest reading is Wagar's: a context action is fine so long as it is the same button every time and does not take the player's other moves away. That argues for context moments in RatQuest that are entered and LEFT freely (he can walk off a stool), not ones that lock him in.
- The Donkey Kong 64 page gives the owner's own instinct ("less collectibles ... each collectible should be more rewarding") a history: the studio that made Conker had just been told "more things", was criticised for it, and its next platformer collected one thing only.
