## 1. Core mechanics and numbers (stamina, rolling, i-frames, poise, parry)

### Takeaway
Only partial, conflicting numbers were found. DS1 roll i-frames are reported as 9/11/13 by equip-load tier (15 with a ring) by one wiki, and as a flat 11 by another source. Treat all frame counts as unverified.

### Cited Findings
- DS1 equip load mainly changes roll recovery and instability frames. One source says rolling gives 11 i-frames and the equip tiers only change recovery. A DS1 wiki page instead lists 9 (slow), 11 (medium), 13 (fast) and 15 with the Dark Wood Grain Ring. The sources conflict, and both are community wikis. — [Dark Souls Wiki: Rolling](https://darksouls.fandom.com/wiki/Rolling), [Slow roll](https://darksouls.fandom.com/wiki/Slow_roll), [Equip Load](https://darksouls.fandom.com/wiki/Equip_Load) (seen via search summary only)
- DS1 stamina regeneration is listed at 45 per second, and equip load above 50% cuts it by 20% (DS1 wiki). — [Dark Souls Wiki: Stamina](https://darksouls.fandom.com/wiki/Stamina) (search summary)
- The DS2 wiki gives about 52 stamina per second at 0% load. DS2 and DS3 are not the subject, so this is context only. — [DS2 Fextralife Combat](https://darksouls2.wiki.fextralife.com/Combat)
- DS3 wiki: light attacks cost less stamina and do less damage, and strong attacks cost more and do more damage. Parries have startup, active and ending frames, and the hit must land during the active frames. DS3 is not the subject. — [DS3 Fextralife Combat](https://darksouls3.wiki.fextralife.com/Combat)
- Poise governs how hard a character is to stagger. — [Dark Souls Wiki: Poise](https://darksouls.fandom.com/wiki/Poise_(Dark_Souls))

### Inferences
- Rolling tied to equipment weight is a build-based trade-off between defence and mobility (inference from the wiki descriptions).

### Gaps
- No verified DS1 numbers for attack wind-up, active and recovery, backstep, guard break, parry window, riposte, backstab, hit-stun, Estus animation length or damage values. No sourced frame-data spreadsheet was reached.
- Animation commitment and the lack of cancelling are not directly sourced here. The only related find is the Critpoints essay (section 4).

## 3. Miyazaki on combat, fairness and difficulty

### Takeaway
Miyazaki frames difficulty as a means to a sense of accomplishment, and fairness as the player understanding why they died. These come from search summaries of interviews and should be re-checked in the originals.

### Cited Findings
- Demon's Souls, Game Informer 2009: being "difficult" was never the goal. Difficulty is one way to give an intense sense of accomplishment through strategy, overcoming obstacles and discovery. — [Game Informer 2009](https://gameinformer.com/games/demons_souls/b/ps3/archive/2009/11/05/feature-demon-s-souls-director-discusses-difficulty-sequels-and-more)
- King's Field "definitely" influenced Demon's Souls, but it was meant as a completely original title. — same Game Informer interview, as paraphrased by the search tool
- Dark Souls core concepts were "sense of achievement" and "surprise of discovery"; high challenge was set to deliver them. He also said play-testing tended to lower difficulty. — [Game Informer Afterwords: Dark Souls (2011)](https://gameinformer.com/b/features/archive/2011/11/12/afterwords-dark-souls)
- Later (Elden Ring era): the design goal is that when players die they can understand why and it feels justified; lowering difficulty would break the game. The search tool attributed this to a Guardian interview, which was not itself seen. — [Game Informer 2024](https://gameinformer.com/interview/2024/06/24/from-softwares-hidetaka-miyazaki-discusses-his-approach-to-difficulty), [GamesRadar](https://www.gamesradar.com/games/action-rpg/hidetaka-miyazaki-says-games-like-elden-ring-have-to-be-hard-if-we-really-wanted-the-whole-world-to-play-the-game-we-could-just-crank-the-difficulty-down)
- Other interviews not yet read: [Gigazine on Dark Souls, 2012](https://gigazine.net/gsc_news/en/20120228-darksouls-miyazaki-interview), [GameBanshee](https://gamebanshee.com/x4af).

### Inferences
- "Justified death" is the developer's own definition of fairness, and it maps to readable tells and consistent rules.

### Gaps
- Nothing found on Miyazaki's "weight" or "realistic sword fighting" remarks, or on why every action has a cost.

## 4. Analysts' principles

### Takeaway
Little sourced material was reached. What exists points to commitment and spacing.

### Cited Findings
- Critpoints essay: being able to move during an action reduces its feeling of commitment, while attacks like the Stinger keep a strong feeling of commitment. The essay is described as unfinished and informal, and is not Dark Souls specific. — [Critpoints: Treatise on Commitment](https://critpoints.net/2015/03/24/treatise-on-commitment-in-action-games/)
- Substack essay: spatial positioning, rather than dodges or counters, is at the heart of Dark Souls combat (compared to NES games). Opinion. — [Substack](https://jmargaris.substack.com/p/dark-souls-its-like-an-nes-game)
- Game Maker's Toolkit: no Dark Souls video was found. A Persian summary of a GMTK "good combat system" video notes that fast combat (Devil May Cry) and slow, tactical combat (Dark Souls) need different systems. Secondhand. — [Digikala summary](https://www.digikala.com/mag/gmtk-good-combat-system/)

### Inferences
- Commitment is presented as a source of weight, so an acrobatic game must decide which actions lock the player in. This is my reading, not a source's claim.

### Gaps
- No Extra Credits, Errant Signal, Matthewmatosis or GDC material reached.

## 5. How FromSoftware sped the formula up (Bloodborne, Sekiro, Elden Ring)

### Takeaway
Miyazaki said Bloodborne was meant to be faster and more aggressive, and that shields made Demon's Souls combat feel passive. The principles kept unchanged could not be established from these results.

### Cited Findings
- Famitsu (as reported): players need to "think much faster" and cannot rely mainly on defence; "if you wait passively to respond, you're going to be eaten." — [Push Square](https://www.pushsquare.com/news/2014/06/e3_2014_ps4_exclusive_bloodborne_will_force_you_to_think_on_your_feet) and other 2014 coverage, per search summary
- 4Gamer: Demon's Souls combat was defined by sword and shield, "particularly shields", and ended up feeling a bit passive. Removing shields was not out of frustration with turtling ("No, not at all"). — [4Gamer translation on NeoGAF](https://www.neogaf.com/threads/bloodborne-4gamer-interview-with-hidetaka-miyazaki-fully-translated.840059/) (not opened; secondhand via search summary)
- Producer Shuhei Yoshida reportedly said shields "might be crap". — [Push Square](https://pushsquare.com/news/2015/03/shields_in_bloodborne_might_be_crap_says_producer)
- Sekiro: Miyazaki said it needs a different approach from Dark Souls, and players who play it like Dark Souls will struggle. Posture and deflect reward deflecting just before a hit. The PlayStation Blog question compared Sekiro's speed to Dark Souls and Bloodborne, but his answer was not retrieved. — [Destructoid](https://vip-develop.destructoid.com/?p=242122), [PlayStation Blog](https://blog.playstation.com/2018/07/13/interview-miyazaki-on-the-limbs-and-lore-of-sekiro-shadows-die-twice/)
- Miyazaki called Sekiro a "turning point" and said offence and defence become "more fluid and active", with games able to get faster still ("one more level we can crank it up to"). — [GamesRadar](https://gamesradar.com/games/action/sekiro-was-a-turning-point-for-fromsoftware-says-hidetaka-miyazaki-who-thinks-the-studios-games-can-still-get-faster-theres-one-more-level-we-can-crank-it-up-to), [PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/games/adventure/sekiro-was-a-big-turning-point-after-elden-ring-hidetaka-miyazaki-says-theres-one-more-level-we-can-crank-it-up-to/)
- Elden Ring: Miyazaki called Sekiro's influence indirect, and one outlet links stance break to Sekiro's posture. The Guard Counter is described by Edge as a concession to defensive players, and jump attacks are compared to charged heavies. These are not attributed to Miyazaki. — [GamesRadar](https://www.gamesradar.com/uk/how-sekiro-influenced-elden-ring-according-to-hidetaka-miyazaki), [Edge via PressReader](https://www.pressreader.com/australia/edge/20220324/283459259823502)
- Possibly useful, unread: [GamesRadar on Elden Ring and shields](https://gamesradar.com/elden-ring-is-miyazakis-most-successful-attempt-to-get-players-out-from-behind-their-shields), [Game Informer 2024 Bloodborne](https://gameinformer.com/interview/2024/06/18/hidetaka-miyazaki-talks-why-bloodborne-is-special-to-him-and-how-it-led-to).

### Inferences
- Shield removal and the later Sekiro mechanics are best read as changing defence from passive blocking to timed, active play, not as removing consequence. This is not confirmed by a quote.

### Gaps
- Quickstep, regain/rally and trick-weapon specifics are not sourced. Miyazaki's own list of principles kept unchanged is not found.

## 6. Character control and known weaknesses

### Takeaway
The recurring complaints are lock-on at the centre of large bosses and camera behaviour near walls. Input buffering is not sourced.

### Cited Findings
- Steam forum posts complain about camera failure near walls, including in Sekiro, and about lock-on bouncing in narrow corridors. Forum posts are anecdotal. — [Steam Sekiro thread](https://steamcommunity.com/app/814380/discussions/0/1815422173046189998), [Steam Lords of the Fallen thread](https://steamcommunity.com/app/265300/discussions/0/4830646290528459041)
- Lock-on targets the centre of a giant model, so the camera struggles with scale and distance on large bosses (DS3 and Elden Ring posts). The lensviewing.com articles read as low quality and should not be relied on. — [Steam Elden Ring thread](https://steamcommunity.com/app/1245620/discussions/0/4343240481093057874)
- Critpoints has a lock-on styles essay, unread. — [Critpoints](https://critpoints.net/2015/05/24/what-is-your-ideal-form-of-lock-on-in-action-games/)

### Inferences
- A fast, acrobatic game will stress camera and lock-on harder than DS1 does, so those need design attention early. This is my inference.

### Gaps
- No sources on turn speed, acceleration, roll distance, jumping (absent in DS1), free versus locked camera, or input buffering and queueing criticism.
