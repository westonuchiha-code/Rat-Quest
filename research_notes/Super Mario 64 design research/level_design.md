## Hub, star counts and gating

### Takeaway
Structure (hub plus star-gated doors, 120 stars) could only be partly verified; exact door thresholds and the per-gate proportions were not confirmed.

### Cited Findings
- Mario Wiki describes eight star doors in the original (nine in the DS version), each needing a different number of Power Stars; the first needs zero. — [Mario Wiki search listing, ★ door](https://www.mariowiki.com/★_door) (via search summary, not fetched)
- Big Star Doors (the 70-star door) are a separate type: the door opens regardless, but a staircase never ends unless Mario has at least 70 stars (80 in the DS remake). — [Mario Wiki, Big Star Door](https://mariowiki.com/Big_Star_Door) (via search summary)
- Bob-omb Battlefield is the first course and the only one with a zero-star entry requirement. — [Mario Wiki, Bob-omb Battlefield](https://www.mariowiki.com/Bob-omb_Battlefield) (via search summary)

### Inferences
- Gates (1, 3, 8, 12, 30, 50, 70 of 120) would be roughly 1%, 2.5%, 7%, 10%, 25%, 42%, 58% of all stars; this is my arithmetic from the thresholds in the brief, which I could NOT verify in a source. Most gates ask well under half the stars, so a player can skip many missions (inference).

## Course case studies (Bob-omb Battlefield, Whomp's Fortress, Cool Cool Mountain, Tick Tock Clock, Tall Tall Mountain, Rainbow Ride, Dire Dire Docks, Hazy Maze Cave)

### Takeaway
Only critic opinion on collectibles/linearity was retrievable; no sourced size, route or mission-reuse detail.

### Cited Findings
- Bob-omb Battlefield: mountainous area; southern territory with Bob-omb Buddies, northern territory with the main mountain; cannons launch Mario across the level; first star from defeating King Bob-omb. — [Mario Wiki / search summary](https://www.mariowiki.com/Bob-omb_Battlefield)
- Critic opinion (Goomba Stomp "Level By Level"): Tall Tall Mountain is "arguably the most linear in the game", most of its stars "feel like clones of Star 1", and its 100-coin star is "incredibly tedious" (one-coin enemies, tough slide). — [Goomba Stomp, Level By Level](https://goombastomp.com/?p=101678) (exact article URL for TTM not confirmed; search result group at https://goombastomp.com/super-mario-64-course-14-tick-tock-clock/ and sibling pages)
- Opinion: Hazy Maze Cave 100-coin hunt is among the worst because of backtracking and dead ends. — [Goomba Stomp](https://goombastomp.com/?p=102141) (URL mapping to this article approximate)
- Opinion: Dire Dire Docks' sparse coins force searching every corner; parts feel like reused Jolly Roger Bay. — [Goomba Stomp](https://goombastomp.com/?p=102337) (URL mapping approximate)
- Opinion: Whomp's Fortress 100-coin star is easier thanks to compact layout and blue coins (5 coins each); Snowman's Land's is manageable thanks to coins in the igloo; Tiny-Huge Island's nearly requires coin-maximizing tricks. — [Goomba Stomp, Tiny-Huge Island](https://goombastomp.com/super-mario-64-course-13-tiny-huge-island/) and [Snowman's Land](https://goombastomp.com/super-mario-64-course-10-snowman-land/)

### Inferences
- Compact, hub-centred courses with dense coins make the 100-coin star pleasant; long linear or maze courses make it a chore (derived from the above opinions).

- Course sizes, routes, verticality, per-mission changes for Cool Cool Mountain, Tick Tock Clock, Rainbow Ride, etc.: not retrieved.

## Developer statements on design process

### Takeaway
Movement came first; courses were built to fit it; the "miniature garden" notion is attested.

### Cited Findings
- Per translated 1996 interviews, Miyamoto said about half the effort went into the core movement/physics, with courses and enemies coming at the very end. — [Shmuplations translation, as summarized in search results](https://daverupert.com/2016/09/super-mario-64-1996-developer-interviews/)
- Nintendo Power Oct 1996 (from Famimaga 64): Miyamoto says Mario's actions came first, then courses were made to fit them; levels designed so a "close enough" jump succeeds; Tezuka said "Currently, we have 32 courses, but the final version may have more", maybe 40 (so the final 15+bonus count was a heavy cut). — [Nintendo Power interview, Zelda Dungeon wiki](https://zeldadungeon.net/wiki/Interview:Nintendo_Power_October_1st_1996)
- Miyamoto reportedly spent months perfecting Mario's friction and weight in a test "garden"; he described levels as "garden dioramas" in a rediscovered Japanese interview; the idea originated in a planned "Super Mario FX" with "an entire world in miniature, like miniature trains". — [Time Extension](https://www.timeextension.com/news/2025/04/a-rare-shigeru-miyamoto-interview-about-the-making-of-mario-64-has-just-surfaced-online) (secondary; summary notes much detail comes from fan write-ups)
- Koizumi's 2007 MIGS talk traced the "miniature garden" lineage from Mario 64 to Galaxy. — [Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/game-platforms/migs-2007-nintendo-s-koizumi-on-the-path-from-garden-to-i-galaxy-i-) (title only; content not read)
- Koizumi did model animation but also camera design, enemies and land forms; the first prototype had Mario and Luigi on a flat field. — Iwata Asks (search summary; exact page not confirmed) https://iwataasks.nintendo.com/interviews/wii/super_mario_galaxy/3/1/
