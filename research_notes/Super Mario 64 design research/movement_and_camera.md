Items still resting only on search-result summaries are marked UNVERIFIED. Units: decomp values are in game units per frame (the game runs logic at 30 fps; positions are in world units, Mario's collision height is ~160 units; angles are s16 where 0x10000 = 360 degrees, so 0x4000 = 90 degrees). ## What is the full move set, and what is each move for?

### Takeaway
The decompiled code confirms a layered ground/air action set (walk/run, jump -> double -> triple, long jump, backflip, side flip, wall kick, ground pound, dive, slide kick, jump kick, twirl, crawl, ledge grab, ceiling hang, pole jump). Jump launch values are fixed in code, so each move has a distinct, designable height/distance/speed profile. Level-design purpose is inferred from those profiles; I found no developer statement mapping moves to level-design roles.

### Cited Findings
- Vertical launch velocity (`vel[1]`) and forward-speed change per move (units/frame, from decomp `set_mario_y_vel_based_on_fspeed` etc.). Single jump 42.0 + 0.25*forwardVel, forwardVel x0.8; double jump 52.0 + 0.25*forwardVel, forwardVel x0.8; triple jump 69.0, forwardVel x0.8; backflip 62.0 with forwardVel set to -16.0; side flip 62.0 with forwardVel 8.0 and facing set to stick direction; wall kick 62.0 with forwardVel at least 24.0; long jump 30.0 with forwardVel x1.5 capped at 48.0; slide kick 12.0 with forwardVel at least 32.0; jump kick 20.0; dive adds +15 to forward speed capped at 48; flying (wing-cap) triple jump 82.0; lava boost 84.0 — [mario.c](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/n64decomp/sm64/master/src/game/mario.c)
- Long jump is flagged "slow" if forward speed is 16 or less before the x1.5 multiplier — [mario.c](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/n64decomp/sm64/master/src/game/mario.c)
- Long jump uses a reduced gravity decrement of 2.0/frame (vs 4.0 default), same as slide kick — [mario_step.c](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/n64decomp/sm64/master/src/game/mario_step.c)
- Wall kick vertical velocity (RESOLVED, moderately confident): `act_air_hit_wall` sets `vel[1] = 52.0f` (airborne.c line ~1077) then calls `set_mario_action(ACT_WALL_KICK_AIR)`; the action-entry code in `mario.c` for `ACT_WALL_KICK_AIR` calls `set_mario_y_vel_based_on_fspeed(m, 62.0f, 0.0f)` and raises forwardVel to at least 24 (mario.c lines ~694-698). The entry code runs after the 52 assignment, so the effective launch is 62 (same as backflip/side flip); the 52 is overwritten. This is my reading of two grep'd line ranges, not a documented statement — [mario.c](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/n64decomp/sm64/master/src/game/mario.c), [mario_actions_airborne.c](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/n64decomp/sm64/master/src/game/mario_actions_airborne.c). The wall-kick also flips facing 180 degrees (0x8000).
- Ground pound: ~10 frames of windup where Mario rises by `20 - 2*actionTimer` per frame (if ceiling clearance), then vel[1] = -50.0 with forward speed zeroed — [mario_actions_airborne.c](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/n64decomp/sm64/master/src/game/mario_actions_airborne.c)
- Dive entry: in jump/double jump, pressing B gives a dive if forwardVel > 28.0, otherwise a jump kick; triple jump, freefall, side flip, wall kick, steep jump and flying triple jump go to dive on B with no speed check; backflip, long jump and twirl have no B-dive; Z (ground pound) has no speed requirement in any of these air actions — [mario_actions_airborne.c](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/n64decomp/sm64/master/src/game/mario_actions_airborne.c)
- Dive pitches down by 0x200 per frame, clamped to -0x2AAA (about -60 degrees) — [mario_actions_airborne.c](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/n64decomp/sm64/master/src/game/mario_actions_airborne.c)
- Crouch slide: jumping from crouch with speed > 10 within the first 30 frames of crouching triggers a long jump; B with speed >= 10 gives a slide kick, otherwise a punch — [mario_actions_moving.c](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/n64decomp/sm64/master/src/game/mario_actions_moving.c)
- Crawling uses `intendedMag x 0.1` target speed, capped at 10 on wall hits; "hold walking" (carrying an object) uses x0.4, heavy objects x0.1 — [mario_actions_moving.c](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/n64decomp/sm64/master/src/game/mario_actions_moving.c)
- Twirl (e.g. after certain spinning dives/ledge sequences in code): yaw velocity targets 0x2000 while A is held, 0x1800 otherwise; no fall damage while twirling — [mario_actions_airborne.c](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/n64decomp/sm64/master/src/game/mario_actions_airborne.c)
- Wing cap: with A held while falling, gravity is reduced (2.0 with correction) and terminal velocity is -37.5; flying actions skip gravity — [mario_step.c](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/n64decomp/sm64/master/src/game/mario_step.c)
- Metal cap in water: gravity 1.6, terminal velocity -16.0; metal-water jump vel 32.0 — [mario_step.c](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/n64decomp/sm64/master/src/game/mario_step.c), [mario.c](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/n64decomp/sm64/master/src/game/mario.c)
- Ledge grab is enabled in the air actions: jump, double jump, hold jump, freefall, hold freefall, side flip, wall kick air, long jump, water jump, top-of-pole jump; NOT in steep jump or twirl. Ceiling hang is enabled only in jump and double jump — [mario_actions_airborne.c](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/n64decomp/sm64/master/src/game/mario_actions_airborne.c)
- Swimming, pole/tree climbing, punch-combo timing and the cap powers' pickup logic: not covered by the files I could read (see Gaps).

- Swimming (decomp, units/frame): max swim speed 28; breaststroke A strokes strength 160 (up to 280 when A released early), holding A converts to flutter kick; buoyancy +1.25 near surface; B does a water punch, not a kick; water jump from surface needs A, pitch >= 0 and stick Y below -60, launches at vel 62; pitch from stick = -252 x stick Y; metal cap buoyancy -18 — [mario_actions_submerged.c](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/n64decomp/sm64/master/src/game/mario_actions_submerged.c)
- Pole climbing: climb up needs stick Y above 16, speed stickY/8 units/frame; slide down max 16 units/frame; A jumps off with a 180-degree wall-kick-style action, A at top gives top-of-pole jump; Z drops — [mario_actions_automatic.c](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/n64decomp/sm64/master/src/game/mario_actions_automatic.c)
- Ledge: fast climb with A or automatically if under 100 units high; slow climb needs input within +-90 degrees of facing; grab needs 160 units ceiling clearance; hang from hangable ceilings moves at up to 4.0 units/frame — [mario_actions_automatic.c](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/n64decomp/sm64/master/src/game/mario_actions_automatic.c)
- Crouch: A from crouch gives backflip; B gives punch (arg 9 trip); analog input starts crawl — [mario_actions_stationary.c](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/n64decomp/sm64/master/src/game/mario_actions_stationary.c)
- Wikipedia move list: walk, run, jump, crouch, crawl, climb, swim, kick, grab, punch; special jumps double, triple, long jump, backflip, wall jump; Wing Cap flight after triple jump or cannon, Metal Cap immune to enemies — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Super_Mario_64)

### Inferences
- Computed from the table, ignoring variable-jump cut and drag: apex height = v^2/(2*g) with g = 4/frame gives roughly 220 units (single, v~42-ish scaling with speed), ~340 (double, v=52), ~595 (triple, v=69) and ~480 (backflip/side flip/wall kick, v=62); long jump (v=30, g=2) gives ~225 but with 1.5x horizontal speed and half gravity, so it trades height for roughly 3x+ the distance of a walking jump. These are my arithmetic on decomp constants, not sourced figures (the discrete per-frame integration shifts them slightly).
- Role mapping (inference): single = default traversal; double = small extra height with some speed kept; triple = the "height + distance" reward move that requires momentum (speed > 20 gate); long jump = distance/speed; backflip and side flip = high, short-distance, direction-reversing recovery and vertical-gap moves; wall kick = vertical chaining in shafts; ground pound = precise landing/enemy-breaking/object trigger; dive = committed forward burst, slide recovery; slide kick = speed maintenance on flat ground.

- Cap power activation logic and tree-climb specifics not read; ukikipedia/MarioWiki still unreachable.
- Wall-kick 52 vs 62: see resolution above; still not cross-checked against ukikipedia.

## Momentum, acceleration, turning, slopes, sliding; air control; windows (coyote time, wall-kick/firsties, ledge grab)

### Takeaway
Ground movement is an accelerate-toward-target model with speed-dependent acceleration (strong at low speed, tapering near the cap), per-surface-class slope and friction constants, and almost no air drag. Windows are tight and frame-based, there is no coyote time in the code I read, and the notorious wall-kick "firsties" is a documented quirk of a missing return.

### Cited Findings
- Stick mapping: `mag = (stickMag/64)^2 * 64`, `intendedMag = mag/2` (mag/8 when squished); `intendedYaw = atan2s(-stickY, stickX) + camera yaw` — so stick direction is camera-relative and magnitude is squared (finer control at low deflection) — [mario.c](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/n64decomp/sm64/master/src/game/mario.c)
- Walking target speed = intendedMag, capped at 32 (24 on slow surfaces). Below target, speed rises each frame by `1.1 - forwardVel/43`; above target on near-flat floor (normal.y >= 0.95) it drops by 1.0 per frame; hard cap 48 — [mario_actions_moving.c](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/n64decomp/sm64/master/src/game/mario_actions_moving.c)
- Starting from rest, walking raises forward speed to min(intendedMag, 8.0) immediately if lower (half when holding an object), except on very slippery floors — [mario.c](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/n64decomp/sm64/master/src/game/mario.c)
- Turn-around/skid starts when the stick is held back (more than about 100 degrees from facing) and speed >= 16; skid deceleration is `apply_slope_decel(2.0)` x class multiplier (very slippery 0.4, slippery 1.4, default 4.0, not slippery 6.0 per frame); turn finishes at speed 8.0; wall hit while braking at speed > 16 bonks backward — [mario_actions_moving.c](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/n64decomp/sm64/master/src/game/mario_actions_moving.c)
- Slope acceleration (down-slope) by class: very slippery 5.3, slippery 2.7, default 1.7, not slippery 0.0. Slide acceleration: 10 / 8 / 7 / 5; slide speed loss factors 0.98 / 0.96 / 0.92 / 0.92; slide speed cap 100 — [mario_actions_moving.c](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/n64decomp/sm64/master/src/game/mario_actions_moving.c)
- Slope thresholds (floor-normal based, approximate angles): "slippery" check ~10 degrees (very slippery), ~20 (slippery), ~38 (default); "slope" check ~5/10/15/20 degrees; "steep" ~15/20/30/30 degrees (only when not facing downhill). Surface angle beyond steep forces Mario to slide/push off (`mario_push_off_steep_floor`: speed +-16) — [mario.c](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/n64decomp/sm64/master/src/game/mario.c), [mario_step.c](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/n64decomp/sm64/master/src/game/mario_step.c)
- Gravity: 4.0 per frame default, terminal velocity -75.0; long jump / slide kick 2.0; shot from cannon 1.0; releasing A while rising with vel > 20 divides vertical velocity by 4 (variable jump height) — [mario_step.c](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/n64decomp/sm64/master/src/game/mario_step.c)
- Air drag: forward speed approaches zero by 0.35 per frame; above a threshold (32, or 48 for long jump) speed additionally drops 1.0 per frame. Air steering: `update_air_without_turn` forward += 1.5 * cos(angle diff) * (intendedMag/32) and a sideways component of 10 scaled likewise; `update_air_with_turn` yaw += 512 * sin * (intendedMag/32). Reverse-speed floor -16 — [mario_actions_airborne.c](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/n64decomp/sm64/master/src/game/mario_actions_airborne.c). Note: mario_step.c itself contains no air drag/horizontal damping; the drag lives in the airborne actions file.
- Physics resolution: air steps split velocity into four quarter steps (`intendedPos = pos + vel/4`); ground steps scale horizontal motion by floor normal.y; gravity applied once after the quarter-step loop; Mario leaves the ground when more than 100 units above floor — [mario_step.c](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/n64decomp/sm64/master/src/game/mario_step.c), [mario.c](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/n64decomp/sm64/master/src/game/mario.c)
- Wall collision probes: air upper radius 150/height 50, lower radius 30/height 50; ground lower radius 30/height 24, upper radius 60/height 50 — [mario_step.c](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/n64decomp/sm64/master/src/game/mario_step.c)
- Jump chain timers: on landing, `doubleJumpTimer` is set to 5 (frames) for single-jump, freefall, double-jump and long-jump landings, 0 for triple-jump and backflip landings. Pressing A in the landing window gives a double jump from a single-jump landing, a jump from a double-jump landing that becomes a triple jump if speed > 20 (single jump otherwise), nothing from a triple-jump landing. Landing lasts 4 frames (6 for long jump) — [mario_actions_moving.c](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/n64decomp/sm64/master/src/game/mario_actions_moving.c), [mario.c](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/n64decomp/sm64/master/src/game/mario.c)
- Wall kick: on impact `wallKickTimer = 5` frames; a kick requires A press, timer non-zero and previous action being air-hit-wall; the "instant wall kick" window is `actionTimer <= 2`. At impact with forward speed >= 38 Mario is knocked backward instead; otherwise speed is capped to -8 and he soft-bonks (the kick can still be done from the bonk) — [mario_actions_airborne.c](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/n64decomp/sm64/master/src/game/mario_actions_airborne.c)
- Firsties: the decomp notes a missing return at the end of `act_air_hit_wall` so the action effectively runs twice on the same frame; the source comment says this makes firsties last one frame instead of two — [mario_actions_airborne.c](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/n64decomp/sm64/master/src/game/mario_actions_airborne.c). (Interpretation of the comment from the tool's summary of the source; the term "firsties" is community jargon I could not confirm elsewhere.)
- Ledge grab (`check_ledge_grab`): requires vel[1] <= 0 and displacement opposing the wall; probes 60 units into the wall normal, searches for floor starting 160 above the next position; grabs only if the ledge floor is more than 100 units above the next position — [mario_step.c](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/n64decomp/sm64/master/src/game/mario_step.c)
- Clearance: Mario's step is blocked if floor + 160 >= ceiling (160 = standing height); squish detection in dynamic gaps 0-150 — [mario_step.c](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/n64decomp/sm64/master/src/game/mario_step.c), [mario.c](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/n64decomp/sm64/master/src/game/mario.c)

### Inferences
- Coyote time: none of the ground/air step code I read grants grace frames after leaving a ledge (leaving ground is simply position > floor+100 / floor lost), so the game appears to have no explicit coyote time; this is an absence-of-evidence inference from partial files, not a confirmed statement.
- The 5-frame (about 0.17 s at 30 fps) landing window for double/triple jumps, 2-frame instant wall-kick window, and 5-frame wall-kick grace timer show the game combines tight chaining windows with some leniency (the 5-frame wall-kick grace and the ability to kick out of a bonk).
- Squared stick magnitude plus `1.1 - v/43` acceleration explains the "weighty but responsive" feel: instant 8-unit start, then a rise that slows as speed nears the cap (asymptotically ~47 from the formula 1.1*43 = 47.3, hence the 48 hard cap).

### Gaps
- Exact long-jump launch speed in a real setting, triple jump chaining across multiple frames beyond these timers, and measured walk/run speeds from video (the CoG 2024 paper "JumpAndRunFlow" reportedly measures Mario 64 acceleration/max speed from footage; could not fetch it).
- No access to ukikipedia/pannenkoek2012 to cross-check "firsties", coyote-like behaviors, or parallel universes.

## How moves chain; skilled vs. beginner routes

### Takeaway
The code makes speed a resource carried between actions: the triple-jump gate (speed > 20), the long jump (needs speed > 10 from crouch), the dive (needs speed > 28 from a jump), and slide kick (speed >= 10) all reward momentum, while low speed degrades gracefully to a plain jump or punch. That structure yields optional faster lines without forcing them.

### Cited Findings
- Triple jump requires forward speed > 20 on the second-jump landing; otherwise the player gets only a single jump — [mario.c](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/n64decomp/sm64/master/src/game/mario.c)
- Long jump from crouch requires speed > 10 within 30 frames of crouching; slide kick requires B with speed >= 10; a speed-kick dive needs stick magnitude > 48 and speed >= 29 — [mario_actions_moving.c](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/n64decomp/sm64/master/src/game/mario_actions_moving.c)
- Slide kick launches with vel 12.0 and forward speed at least 32.0 at half gravity, i.e. a speed-conserving move — [mario.c](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/n64decomp/sm64/master/src/game/mario.c), [mario_step.c](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/n64decomp/sm64/master/src/game/mario_step.c)
- Wall kick vertical velocity is higher than a double jump and forward speed at least 24, so wall kicks chain upward — [mario.c](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/n64decomp/sm64/master/src/game/mario.c)
- Air speed is uncapped by the engine's own comment ("Uncapped air speed. Net positive when moving forward.") — [mario_actions_airborne.c](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/n64decomp/sm64/master/src/game/mario_actions_airborne.c)

### Inferences
- A beginner can complete levels with single jumps, stomps and walking; advanced routes (triple jump, long jump, wall-kick shafts, dive-slide recovery) cut time/distance. No developer quote confirming this was intentional design was obtained.

### Gaps
- No developer or analyst source captured on intended difficulty layering or on speedrun-route examples; these require ukikipedia/speedrun wiki access.

## Developer statements on feel (analog stick, weight, animation, sound)

### Takeaway
First-hand reads of the 1996 Japanese strategy-guide interviews show Nintendo built the 3D stick control in a bare block room first ("once that felt smooth, we knew we were halfway there"), deliberately accepted slow acceleration and a "close enough" jump design, tuned a weighty physical feel with hand-made animation, and made movements for every button combination.

### Cited Findings
- Origin: "We were trying to get the controls right with an analogue 3D stick, and once that felt smooth, we knew we were halfway there." The prototype was "a room made of simple lego-like blocks" where Mario and Luigi "could run around... climb slopes, jump around" — Miyamoto, [shmuplations 1996 interviews](https://shmuplations.com/mario64/)
- Core fun: "it was being able to move Mario and Luigi around with the 3D control stick, and being able to change the camera view with the press of a button... We wanted to make a game where just moving Mario around was fun." Also: "we get the fundamentals solid first, then do as much with that core concept as our time and ambition will allow." About half of development time went into the basic system; courses and enemies "came at the very end... done in a single burst of energy" — Miyamoto, [shmuplations](https://shmuplations.com/mario64/)
- Jumping/3D design change: in earlier Marios "we were able to measure the number of pixels Mario could jump", but "this time, we had to design the levels so that as long as your jump was 'close enough', you'd make it; it was too hard for the player to judge. This was a design change we made in the middle of the development... There was a lot of booing from the staff." Interviewer: "jumping became more intuitive, and less quantitative"; Miyamoto: "Exactly." — [shmuplations](https://shmuplations.com/mario64/)
- Weight and skeleton: motion capture was tried "but ultimately we ended up doing it all by hand." Mario's centre of gravity is around the hips, "a big 'joint' that controls which way his body moves... when he accelerates and inclines forward, when he turns and leans left or right... So Mario sort of runs like Arale-chan, with the correct sense of weight in the body." — Miyamoto, [shmuplations](https://shmuplations.com/mario64/)
- Deliberate cheating: on the long jump being faster than running, "With 3D, little 'lies' like that can go unnoticed. So we lied a lot!" — Miyamoto, [shmuplations](https://shmuplations.com/mario64/)
- Animation volume: "193 different animation patterns... if you include the 50 or so animations that we created but ultimately rejected, it comes to nearly 250" (Nishida). Miyamoto's guidance was only "create as many different movements as they could"; "as players got good at the controls, they'd want to try out more and more button combinations... So we created movements for all button combinations—of course, that means a few useless ones got left in too." Koizumi: the "crouching trip kick... was made to deal with short, small enemies, but we ended up not adding those kinds of enemies" — [shmuplations](https://shmuplations.com/mario64/)
- Animation serving gameplay: Koizumi: "It's easy to design jumps, since Mario needs them to reach a certain ledge of a certain distance... But his sleeping animation... isn't connected to anything else in the game, so it was harder." Also the team added animations on their own (e.g. jump from crouch) — [shmuplations](https://shmuplations.com/mario64/)
- Slow acceleration was a conscious bet: at the Nov 1995 expo "a lot of people say 'I don't know about this, the controls feel really wobbly and slippery...' We weren't about to back down... 'walking around leisurely can be fun too!'... I was a little worried... normally games have a faster pace... it's reflected in the controls, and how long it takes to accelerate." Miyamoto added "We tightened the springs a bit on Mario, maybe? I don't know." — [shmuplations](https://shmuplations.com/mario64/)
- Physics is tuned not real: Nishida: "It's not actually 'real' physics... Gravity, friction, resistance... have properly set parameters, but if you rely on the actual laws of physics too much it doesn't really make for a good game. The floatiness of Mario's jump... works great in a game". Miyamoto: moving "like a car, or like a human, or like an airplane... all those require different physics calculations. His different jumps all require different calculations too." — [shmuplations](https://shmuplations.com/mario64/)
- Exploration play: children playtesters spent half a day on King Bob-omb's stage repeatedly trying an "unclimbable hill"; Miyamoto: "if a game was this fun to play even if you weren't getting anywhere, well, it must be alright." — [shmuplations](https://shmuplations.com/mario64/)
- Dash button removed: Miyamoto says they tested holding a button to dash but, with "the N64 controller... already large" and small children playing, plus "guilt... forcing players to press two buttons simultaneously... we removed the hold-button-to-dash option entirely" — [shmuplations Miyamoto x Eno](https://shmuplations.com/miyamotoxeno/)
- Eno on the jump: Eno says Mario's 2D jump feels like it gets "a slight extra bit of distance at the very end" if A is held (Eno's perception, in the interview), and that the 3D stick fulfilled the ideal of the B-Dash — [shmuplations Miyamoto x Eno](https://shmuplations.com/miyamotoxeno/)
- Voice: Mario's voice was a professional voice actor recorded years earlier; "Mario talks a lot more in the American version... 'Okie Dokie!'" — Miyamoto, [shmuplations](https://shmuplations.com/mario64/). Developers did not discuss voice as control feedback in these interviews.
- Koizumi (MIGS 2007): "His first step was Mario's first step – it began with making the character walk"; controlling the character felt "new and fresh"; Miyamoto acted out animations himself to correct them — [Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/game-platforms/migs-2007-nintendo-s-koizumi-on-the-path-from-garden-to-i-galaxy-i-)
- Koizumi on depth perception: "We decided to drop a shadow on the ground everywhere in Mario 64... every floating object would have a reference point on the ground", with the shadow directly below regardless of light source: "It might not be realistic, but it's much easier to play with the shadow directly below" — [Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/game-platforms/migs-2007-nintendo-s-koizumi-on-the-path-from-garden-to-i-galaxy-i-)
- Frame-keyed sounds per move in code (triple jump frames 2, 8, 20; backflip 2, 3, 17; side flip 6); punch sounds "yah"/"wah" tied to combo stages — [mario_actions_airborne.c](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/n64decomp/sm64/master/src/game/mario_actions_airborne.c), [mario_actions_object.c](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/n64decomp/sm64/master/src/game/mario_actions_object.c)
- Wikipedia (secondary): development "began with the characters and the camera system"; "The development team prioritized Mario's movement and, before levels were created, tested and refined Mario's animations on a simple grid" — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Super_Mario_64)

### Inferences
- The "close enough" jump policy plus slow-to-accelerate stick plus forgiving 5-frame grace timers suggest a coherent philosophy: forgive imprecision in space, ask for skill in momentum management. Miyamoto only states the first part.
- Per-move jump physics (distinct launch velocities/gravity per jump) match the "different calculations" remark.

### Gaps
- Nothing found where developers describe Mario's voice as deliberate input feedback, or discuss the wall kick, ground pound or dive specifically. The earlier claim of a "garden" tuned for months for friction/weight came from an aggregator summary and I did not find it in the shmuplations text; the Koizumi MIGS piece uses "miniature garden" for world design, and Swink's "Magic Garden" refers to a test environment, so that earlier claim is UNVERIFIED and probably conflated.

## Camera (Lakitu, C-buttons, modes, tight spaces, failings)

### Takeaway
The camera is a diegetic "Lakitu cameraman" with many modes. Developers framed it as game design and a major goal; Miyamoto admitted users find it chaotic; critics split.

### Cited Findings
- Modes in the decomp: level-oriented (radial, outward radial, 8 directions at 45-degree steps, fixed, spiral stairs, parallel tracking, boss fight) and Mario-oriented (behind Mario, default close/free-roam, Mario camera, C-Up, cannon, slide) — [camera.c](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/n64decomp/sm64/master/src/game/camera.c)
- C-buttons: C-Up first-person look (head turn clamped about 80 degrees up / 45 down), C-Left/C-Right rotate about 90 degrees (45 in 8-direction mode), C-Down zooms out; each has a sound and buzzers mark disabled input — [camera.c](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/n64decomp/sm64/master/src/game/camera.c)
- Lakitu's yaw approaches its target at 0x400 per frame; default mode camera lags at most 50 units behind its goal — [camera.c](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/n64decomp/sm64/master/src/game/camera.c)
- Wall handling: `rotate_camera_around_walls()` at 0x600 yaw rate, about twice as fast near a wall and 0x1000 when Mario within 250 units; probe `collide_with_walls(cPos, 10, 80)`; camera 125 units above floor; C-Up exit searches 16 sectors of 22.5 degrees — [camera.c](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/n64decomp/sm64/master/src/game/camera.c)
- Zoom: default 800; Mario cam 350; C-Up 250; radial base 1000; boss 800-5000; zoom step 30/frame; fixed-camera `scaleToMario` 0.5 default, 0 in Rainbow Ride and Big Boo's Haunt, 0.3 castle lobby. Only the first ~100k of ~403k characters of camera.c were read — [camera.c](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/n64decomp/sm64/master/src/game/camera.c)
- Koizumi (MIGS 2007): Miyamoto says "the greatest appeal of working on 3d games is the camera"; "During Mario 64, we had to ask, 'what kind of camera is best suited for this gameplay?'"; options considered were parallel camera (closest to 2D, easiest not to get lost), tower camera (straight-on side view) and follow camera; "Thinking about the camera is game design, too." — [Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/game-platforms/migs-2007-nintendo-s-koizumi-on-the-path-from-garden-to-i-galaxy-i-)
- Koizumi: three 3D problems were depth perception, getting lost and motion sickness; for getting lost "we included large landmarks so players could orient themselves"; camera pitch on jumping "can make some people ill" and Nintendo used a "vertical shake cushion" (camera won't pan vertically while Mario is near screen centre); of free camera control in Sunshine and Ocarina: "the overall control scheme became too complex... we divided the group of people that might have enjoyed the game" — [Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/game-platforms/migs-2007-nintendo-s-koizumi-on-the-path-from-garden-to-i-galaxy-i-). (The "vertical shake cushion" is described in the context of Mario games generally in this article, not tied specifically to SM64.)
- Miyamoto to Eno (1996): "we get a lot of users saying it moves around chaotically on its own and makes the game hard to play, or that they could design a better camera themselves." Eno praised it as "a magnificent answer to the problem of the camera" but found boss-fling/stage-clear staging clunky ("The Camera in Mario 64 Explains Too Much") — [shmuplations Miyamoto x Eno](https://shmuplations.com/miyamotoxeno/)
- Miyamoto: the team's early spec had onlookers shouting "move the camera over there!"; "I want a game that allows players to try come up with their own solutions" — [shmuplations](https://shmuplations.com/mario64/). Tanimoto/Miyamoto note Z-buffer reduced restrictions like "don't make the camera do this".
- Wikipedia: camera programmed by Takumi Kawagoe using Star Fox 2 experience; original concept was a fixed isometric path (like Super Mario RPG); development began with camera and characters; camera shown as Lakitu with C-button control; reviews mixed: Next Generation cited "occasional erratic movements and lack of optimal angle"; Radakovic and Perry said the camera was sometimes blocked by or went through objects; EGM reviewers each removed half a point because it could not reach a wanted angle or shifted rapidly — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Super_Mario_64)
- Third-person camera essay (Buehler, Game Developer) states "the camera was integrated into the game as a character: Lakitu" (citing Nitsche 2008) — [Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/third-person-camera-view-in-games-a-record-of-the-most-common-problems-in-modern-games-solutions-taken-from-new-and-retro-games)
- Thompson: "areas such as camera controls would merit further iteration, aspects of visual design, character animation, player movement, level and mission construction set the template" — [Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/game-platforms/analysing-the-ai-of-super-mario-64)

### Inferences
- Per-area fixed/radial modes and `scaleToMario` values show level authors assigned camera behaviour by hand for awkward spaces. No source found that explicitly says geometry was shaped to suit the camera, though Koizumi's landmarks and "large" spaces point that way (inference).

### Gaps
- AV Club and GMTK camera analyses unreadable; no first-hand statement on why Lakitu was chosen (the earlier Nintendojo claim is unverified).

## Combat, enemies and bosses

### Takeaway
There is no separate combat system. Attacks are movement actions with attack flags, enemies are built around stomp/punch/throw/ground-pound, and the three bosses read from the decomp are defined by grab-and-throw (King Bob-omb, Bowser) or ground pound (Whomp). Hits needed: 3, 1-3, 3.

### Cited Findings
- Punch combo (`mario_actions_object.c`): `actionTimer` starts at 7 and indexes forward velocities {0,1,1,2,3,5,7,10}; sequence is punch ("yah", punch flag from anim frame 2), second punch ("wah"), then ground kick (kicking flag frames 0-7); B pressed during the fast phase advances the chain; holding A from a fresh punch gives a jump kick; breakdance trip (arg 9, from crouch, tripping flag frames 2-7) — [mario_actions_object.c](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/n64decomp/sm64/master/src/game/mario_actions_object.c)
- Throwing: ground throw releases at action timer 7, heavy throw at 13, placing down at 8; Bowser hold spins with stick, angular velocity capped at +-0x1000, auto-releases after about 120 idle frames — [mario_actions_object.c](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/n64decomp/sm64/master/src/game/mario_actions_object.c)
- Jump kick: B in the air with forward speed 28 or less, kicking flag frames 0-7, wall hit zeros forward speed; dive: B with forward speed above 28, landing into dive slide; ground pound land: mist/star particles and camera shake — [mario_actions_airborne.c](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/n64decomp/sm64/master/src/game/mario_actions_airborne.c)
- King Bob-omb: health 3; only damaged by being grabbed and thrown so that he lands on the hill (more than 100 units below home counts as no hit and he walks back); escape-grab counter above 10 frees Mario; chase speed 3.0 — [king_bobomb.inc.c](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/n64decomp/sm64/master/src/game/behaviors/king_bobomb.inc.c)
- King Whomp: health 3; vulnerable only in the on-ground state after his jump/land (about 100 frames), each ground pound decrements health; chase speed 9 within 1500 units, jump trigger within 300, jump launch velocity 40, patrol speed 3; small Whomp ground-pounded gives 5 yellow coins — [whomp.inc.c](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/n64decomp/sm64/master/src/game/behaviors/whomp.inc.c)
- Bowser: Mario touches tail to make it grabbable, spins him with the stick and releases; throw speed derives from swing speed and pitch (swing speed x2.5 above 90); hit if he passes within 800 units of a bomb mine while thrown; health 1 (Dark World, Fire Sea) or 3 (Sky); attack choice (fire breath, jumps, teleport) is chosen by distance to Mario — [bowser.inc.c](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/n64decomp/sm64/master/src/game/behaviors/bowser.inc.c)
- Enemy AI is simple: each behaviour object computes distance and angle to Mario first; Goomba chases within ~500 units after a jump, Koopa runs away within 300, enemies wander within a "home" radius; all enemies have two cylindrical hitboxes (object and hurtbox); Mario is 161 units tall in-game; objects have a draw distance and rooms only update when Mario is in the same room — Thompson, [Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/game-platforms/analysing-the-ai-of-super-mario-64)
- Miyamoto: "Normally we'd include about 80 different enemies, but this time we had just under 40. And many of them are neither friend nor foe, but something in-between" — [shmuplations](https://shmuplations.com/mario64/)
- Eno criticised that hitting a clam or walls "I just took standard damage every time" (generic damage response); Miyamoto replied the game's demonstrated aim was free movement in 3D — [shmuplations Miyamoto x Eno](https://shmuplations.com/miyamotoxeno/)
- Hitbox height 100 for short-hitbox actions, 160 otherwise — [mario.c](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/n64decomp/sm64/master/src/game/mario.c)

### Inferences
- Bosses test three different move families: Whomp = ground pound after kiting (aerial precision), King Bob-omb = grab/throw positioning, Bowser = grab and rotate with stick (analog control as the attack). This supports "bosses built from movement", though no developer states it.
- Hits-to-win of 3 is a consistent boss template in these files.

### Gaps
- Attack speed in frames for punches beyond the above; stomp bounce velocity; enemy-specific vulnerabilities (the crouching trip kick intended for small enemies is the only developer statement).
- Level-by-level enemy placement rationale.

## Design analyses (Swink, GMTK, GDC, Game Developer)

### Takeaway
Swink's feature uses SM64 to explain how constraints (object placement) define sensation, and Thompson's source analysis frames SM64's lasting influence; GMTK, GDC and pannenkoek2012 sources could not be reached.

### Cited Findings
- Swink's Game Feel breaks feel into six categories: input, response, context, polish, metaphor, rules, with SM64 as the example; quoted: "If Mario has nothing to interact with, the fact that he has these acrobatic abilities is meaningless. Without a wall, there can be no wall kick... constraints define sensation. If objects are packed in, spaced tightly relative to the avatar's motion, the game will feel clumsy and oppressive... As objects get spaced further apart, the feel becomes increasingly trivialized"; recommends a test environment, "the Magic Garden of game feel" — [Game Developer news summary of Swink's Gamasutra feature](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/game-platforms/feature-game-feel---the-secret-ingredient-). The full feature text was not read.
- Wikipedia: the analog stick "offering more precise and wide-ranging character movements than the digital D-pads"; the game's controls are "fully analog and interpret a 360-degree range of motion into navigation through a 3D space relative to the camera"; "The analog stick allows for precise control over subtleties such as running speed" — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Super_Mario_64)
- Thompson: SM64 "set the standard 3D games at a critical phase in the industry", citing Tim Schafer (Psychonauts) and Michael John (Spyro) as influenced — [Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/game-platforms/analysing-the-ai-of-super-mario-64)
- Wikipedia lists speedrun techniques: Lakitu skip, bloated Bob-omb glitch, backwards long jump (BLJ) on steep slope or stairs for very high speed — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Super_Mario_64)

### Inferences
- The decomp's uncapped air speed ("Uncapped air speed. Net positive when moving forward.") is what the BLJ exploits (inference, linking two sources).
