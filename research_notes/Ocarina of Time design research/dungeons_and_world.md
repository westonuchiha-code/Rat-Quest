## 1. World structure: Hyrule Field hub, child/adult split, optional content, Field size/emptiness/Epona

### Takeaway
Developers describe Hyrule Field as deliberately huge (for horse riding), then found it too empty and patched it with enemies and holes; critics still call it barren. Miyamoto's early fallback was a Ganon's-Castle-only Mario 64-style hub. Region gating and optional-content design (heart pieces, grottos, Skulltulas) are not discussed in the developer sources I could read.

### Cited Findings
Developer statements
- Miyamoto, on the earliest concept: "at first I thought only having Ganon's Castle might be enough... I thought about putting all kinds of adventures into the different rooms, like making a dark meadow or an ocean - like in Princess Peach's Castle in Super Mario 64." "In the worst case, Link wouldn't have been able to go outside the castle!" Context: memory on N64 was fixed, "we started by making the system" rather than the story. — [Iwata Asks Vol. 5, p1](https://www.nintendo.com/en-gb/Iwata-Asks/Iwata-Asks-The-Legend-of-Zelda-Ocarina-of-Time-3D/Vol-5-Mr-Shigeru-Miyamoto/1-Ganon-s-Castle-as-the-Only-Setting-/1-Ganon-s-Castle-as-the-Only-Setting-224548.html)
- Miyamoto on scale: "no one knew how big in scale it would become." Same page.
- Tezuka: "At first we had planned to make the game on a smaller scale, but when we finished it we realized how much larger it was than we had expected." He puts a first playthrough without a guide at about 60 hours. Osawa: Hyrule Field is "around the Imperial Palace, Death Mountain is like Mount Hiei", i.e. the world is Kyoto-scale and has over 200 characters. — [1101 "Tough to Fix (Part 2)"](https://glitterberri.com/ocarina-of-time/1101-interviews/tough-to-fix-part-2/)
- Onozuka (dungeon design) on the horse: it began with Miyamoto saying "I want to put in a horse"; "In the beginning we put in so many trees, but they were becoming obstacles so we gradually removed them and in the end the wilderness disappeared. It feels good to grow up and ride a horse through the wide world you discovered as a child." On the map: "'Just make it big,' we were told!" and there was "no way we could have implemented enemies as well". — [1101 "Link's Horse"](https://glitterberri.com/ocarina-of-time/1101-interviews/links-horse/)
- Miyanaga (field design), same page: "Miyamoto directed us to decrease the obstacles in order to make riding more fun. However, decreasing all the obstacles actually caused more difficulty. For a long time Hyrule Field had nothing, it was terribly uninteresting." Wind, temperature and atmosphere effects were planned for the empty plain.
- Miyanaga (Iwata Asks): "It created quite a fuss when I first made Hyrule Field. People would say, 'You can't make it that big!'... Even riding a horse, it was so big that you would get bored riding around it, so we had to add something. Then lots of people took a hand in it, having enemies appear and putting holes here and there." Aonuma: "We went around Hyrule Field looking for places where there wasn't anything and put something in each one." Miyanaga: "This area's a bit empty, so I'll make a hole and put something in it." Iwata: the field was "a basis for everyone to build their ideas upon". Takizawa: "We've never really had a perfect blueprint when making a Zelda game." — [Iwata Asks Vol. 3, "Making the Landscape"](https://www.nintendo.com/en-gb/Iwata-Asks/Iwata-Asks-The-Legend-of-Zelda-Ocarina-of-Time-3D/Vol-3-Original-Development-Staff-Part-2/5-Making-the-Landscape/5-Making-the-Landscape-216494.html)
- Same page: Kakariko was built up without a blueprint ("If we have this kind of resident, they would live in this kind of house"), and the extension of the release date was welcomed: Miyanaga "I can put a hole at that one empty spot!"
- Miyanaga on atmosphere: wanted "air and atmosphere and wind and smell and temperature"; N64 fog tuned subtly to get "this is morning". — [1101 "Parts They Want You to See (Part 2)"](https://glitterberri.com/ocarina-of-time/1101-interviews/parts-they-want-you-to-see-part-2/)
- Miyamoto: the horse was chosen "because we wanted the vehicle... to be something that brought you into contact with others"; Epona scenes came from long discussions with Koizumi. — [shmuplations 1999 interview](https://shmuplations.com/ocarinaoftime/)
- Koizumi (Iwata Asks) says horse talk began during Mario 64 development; he named her Epona; the game was first made for the 64DD and moved to cartridge because 500 Link motion patterns could not stream from magnetic disk. Young Link was made by scaling Adult Link's model and motions (the animation work was still 1.5 times as much), added about 1.5 years before release. — [Iwata Asks Vol. 2, "What We Couldn't Do with Ocarina of Time"](https://www.nintendo.com/en-gb/Iwata-Asks/Iwata-Asks-The-Legend-of-Zelda-Ocarina-of-Time-3D/Vol-2-Original-Development-Staff-Part-1/5-What-We-Couldn-t-Do-with-Ocarina-of-Time/5-What-We-Couldn-t-Do-with-Ocarina-of-Time-231818.html)
- Onozuka on child/adult in dungeons: "In this game you have the option of being a child or an adult. Thusly, we considered such things as how much money you would be able to carry as a child". — [1101 "Dungeons (Part 2)"](https://glitterberri.com/ocarina-of-time/1101-interviews/dungeons-part-2/)

Reference / analysis
- Wikipedia: Hyrule Field is "the central hub of the world", connected to outlying regions (Kokiri Forest, Zora River, Hyrule Castle town, Kakariko, etc.); optional side quests include the 10-item trading sequence ending in the Biggoron Sword; a horse can be acquired. — [Wikipedia: OoT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Legend_of_Zelda:_Ocarina_of_Time)
- Analysis: a 20th-anniversary retrospective calls Hyrule Field "painfully barren" and "very little more than a way to get from Point A to Point B", and says the child section (roughly a third of the game) has tedious backtracking and some ambiguous progression. — [Game Developer: OoT 20th Anniversary Retrospective](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/game-platforms/ocarina-of-time-20th-anniversary-retrospective)

### Inferences
- The development order was horse and large space first, content second ("go around looking for empty places"). For a hub-and-levels project: budget for filling a large traversal space late, or accept it as connective tissue.
- Miyamoto's stated purpose for Epona is social contact (travel that brings you to others), which ties the hub to the towns.

### Gaps
- Nothing read on how items/songs gate regions, how the field directs the player, grotto and heart piece placement, or the child/adult time-split rationale from the developers.

## 2. Dungeon structure and case studies

### Takeaway
Developers say the early "living" dungeons (Deku Tree, Jabu-Jabu) skip keys, while the man-made temples use them; rooms were sized to Link's body and laid out on CAD "like houses"; the Forest Temple was the first dungeon designed and its twisting hall depends on 3D. Critics supply the per-dungeon structure.

### Cited Findings
Developer statements
- Onozuka: Deku Tree "wasn't made as a place that had sprung into existence, but rather as a thing that had always been there". Dungeons normally require keys, "but at the beginning this isn't required"; Jabu-Jabu's "valves in his gut" instead; "it would be a little weird if you needed keys in the inside of a fish". Mid-game dungeons are man-made temples (Forest, Water, Fire, plus Shadow and Spirit "constructed with 2 sections so you can come and go as either adult or child"). — [1101 "Dungeons (Part 1)"](https://glitterberri.com/ocarina-of-time/1101-interviews/dungeons/)
- Onozuka, process: level blueprints, then designers worked out details; "We ended up planning them like houses... The size of the dungeon rooms was based off the size of Link's body." Things inside dungeons move; some ideas were abandoned for schedule; dungeon material was reused across dungeons. Same page.
- Onozuka on puzzles: likes "old-style Zelda dungeons in which you have to solve puzzles"; to avoid frustration "we make sure to run the puzzle through from the beginning and reduce the number of enemies near it". Progression: "orthodox till the middle of the game, and thereafter you must put the things you've learned to use. At the beginning, there's only one puzzle room to solve, then eventually entire dungeons become rotating puzzles. In the final dungeons, everything you've learned comes into play and the puzzles are accompanied by an athletic element, as the staff who worked on Mario were kind enough to help us out." Same page.
- Onozuka, Forest Temple: "the very first dungeon we designed in Ocarina of Time." "At the center of the Forest Temple lies the twisting pathway. When you go through it, you can reach items you couldn't before. We wouldn't have been able to make something like that if the game hadn't been 3D... the designers... spent about a week tormenting themselves"; he drew them a picture. — [1101 "Dungeons (Part 2)"](https://glitterberri.com/ocarina-of-time/1101-interviews/dungeons-part-2/)
- Onozuka, Water Temple: "the player was required to solve puzzles in not just one room, but travel countless times to and from an area in order to unlock the solution. We were often told how stressful this was. I just wanted the player to relax and take it easy... I'm a fan of skin diving". Also an individually calculated hookshot climb at the waterfall. Fire Temple: "we created a huge dungeon" meant for playing around. Same page. (Attribution caveat: this translation credits "Eiji Onozuka (Dungeon Design)"; Wikipedia and Game Developer credit Eiji Aonuma as dungeon designer and say he created the Water Temple. The names may be the same person transliterated differently; I could not confirm. UNVERIFIED identity.)
- Onozuka, Water Temple rework: "we totally redid the Water Temple. The amount of time Link could stay underwater was changed part way through, so we were working all the way up till this summer to fix situations where an item had become awkwardly positioned". Link's abilities (hookshot, speed) changed midway so mappers had to redo maps. — [1101 "Tough to Fix (Part 2)"](https://glitterberri.com/ocarina-of-time/1101-interviews/tough-to-fix-part-2/)
- Miyamoto: "In every Zelda development, the dungeons take a huge amount of time to make... in the original Legend of Zelda, at the beginning of the development it was just dungeons" ("Dungeon Supremacy"), but for OoT "for the first time we didn't spend as much time on the dungeons". OoT dungeons "are not further iterations on the 'labyrinth' ideas from A Link to the Past"; linear mazes were judged "not really that much fun"; "Instead of mapping your way through a maze... a sense of dread, a sense of pressure, and of course an opportunity for finding secrets and solving puzzles". "There are still traditional mazes, like Gerudo's Fortress and the Forest Temple, but overall I don't think those are very appropriate to a 3D game." — [shmuplations 1999 interview](https://shmuplations.com/ocarinaoftime/)
- Miyamoto supervised "everything up through the first Deku Tree dungeon, like where you destroy the spiderweb and jump down" very closely, and added "little traps and puzzles that, once solved, make the player feel like 'Ah, now this is a Zelda game.'" Same page.
- Osawa (Iwata Asks Vol. 2): even late, items were moved between dungeons ("that item in that one dungeon? ... we need to use that in another dungeon"). Paraphrase from search summary only; page not read in full. UNVERIFIED wording.

Analysis (critics)
- Deku Tree: "one large, open central area" emphasizing vertical space; introduces map, compass and the key item needed for the boss; the leap through the web is a memorable moment. Dodongo's Cavern: "much more traditional... spreads outwards, and only has 2 floors", bombs as the item. Forest Temple: rooms/corridors twist so "what used to be the floor is now the wall", opening new passages; one of few dungeons with outdoor areas; ranked 1st by this critic. Fire Temple: large rooms lacking landmarks, "easy to get turned around". Water Temple: slowness of raising/lowering water and clunky underwater movement, but "some of the best moments", centered on the hookshot. — [Game Developer: Dungeons Ranked Part 1](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/audio/ocarina-of-time-dungeons-ranked-part-1), [Part 2](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/audio/ocarina-of-time-dungeons-ranked-part-2)
- Water Temple: "the puzzle is the dungeon itself as it deeply tests the players spacial sense and memorization skills" (retrospective). — [Game Developer retrospective](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/game-platforms/ocarina-of-time-20th-anniversary-retrospective)
- Water Temple reception: Edge felt challenge came from navigation rather than puzzle difficulty; Eurogamer suggested novelty of 3D contributed; Game Informer and Inverse disliked it; Gameological called it "exhausting repetition". The 3DS version added directions and easier Iron Boots; Aonuma apologised for the experience while saying it was frustrating more than difficult. — [Wikipedia: Water Temple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Water_Temple_(Ocarina_of_Time))
- Spirit Temple and Ganon's Castle: no usable analysis found in readable sources. Spirit Temple child/adult halves confirmed only by Onozuka's "2 sections" remark above.

### Inferences
- The "learn, then test, then combine" curve is stated outright by the dungeon designer (one puzzle room, then rotating-puzzle dungeons, then everything plus athletics).
- Sizing rooms to Link's body and prototyping on CAD is a concrete level-metric practice worth copying.
- The Water Temple's problems are partly a pipeline story: moving rules (underwater time, abilities) changed after maps existed.

### Gaps
- Standard map/compass/small key/boss key structure is only touched by the Deku Tree critic; no developer source on it.
- Detailed Dodongo's Cavern, Spirit Temple, Ganon's Castle design: not found.

## 3. Teaching without text

### Takeaway
Best evidence is Onozuka's difficulty ramp and Miyamoto's personal supervision of the opening through the Deku Tree. The rest is critic commentary.

### Cited Findings
- Early dungeons: simple puzzles, lots of grass hearts to avoid early frustration; later "limited supply". — [1101 "Dungeons (Part 2)"](https://glitterberri.com/ocarina-of-time/1101-interviews/dungeons-part-2/)
- Onozuka: Navi exists because in 3D "it's important that the player be able to see what routes to follow and not overlook something important. By changing the camera viewpoint just a little you might not be able to see something you ought." — [1101 "Half Done by Mario"](https://glitterberri.com/ocarina-of-time/1101-interviews/half-done-by-mario/)
- Miyamoto called Navi "the biggest weakpoint" and said hint design is hard because needed hints "vary from person-to-person". — [shmuplations](https://shmuplations.com/ocarinaoftime/)
- Deku Tree critic: teaches map, compass, key item "in a really short period of time" but Navi interrupts too often. — [Game Developer Part 1](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/audio/ocarina-of-time-dungeons-ranked-part-1)
- Analysis of Ice Cavern: key item not used to solve its puzzles; Gerudo Training Ground "a series of disconnected puzzle rooms" (summary from search results; page body not fully read). UNVERIFIED detail.

### Inferences
- Sight lines to unreachable things and revisiting rooms with new items are not discussed by any developer source read; the Forest Temple twisting hall ("reach items you couldn't before") is the nearest developer-confirmed example of revisiting.

### Gaps
- Boss Keys OoT episode (introduce/test/combine framing) not read.

## 4. Pacing, secrets, rewards, collectibles

### Takeaway
Developers discuss resource placement (jars, rupees) more than secrets; the Skulltula criticism is from critics.

### Cited Findings
- Onozuka: jars with hearts so players need not leave; "lots of rupee-filled treasure chests at first, but people ended up getting annoyed wondering why there were so many rupees in a place where they were totally useless". Hardest question: "what could we put in, at what point in time, in what place, that would make the player happy?" Wallet size and money varied by progress, so they "put a lot of jars around". — [1101 "Dungeons (Part 2)"](https://glitterberri.com/ocarina-of-time/1101-interviews/dungeons-part-2/)
- Onozuka: fewer enemies near puzzles so success feels good. — [1101 "Dungeons (Part 1)"](https://glitterberri.com/ocarina-of-time/1101-interviews/dungeons/)
- Critics on Skulltulas (search-snippet level, pages not read): 100-token reward is 200 rupees; hunt "arduous"; MM's two Spider Houses seen as better. UNVERIFIED wording. — [ScreenRant](https://screenrant.com/zelda-ocarina-time-gold-skulltula-reward-bad-stupid/), [ScreenRant MM comparison](https://screenrant.com/zelda-gold-skultulla-ocarina-time-majoras-mask/)

### Inferences
- The rupee-chest anecdote is a direct developer lesson: a reward must be useful at the place it is given.

### Gaps
- No developer source on Skulltula or heart piece design intent.

## 5. What developers said about development

### Takeaway
Shared lineage with Mario 64 (initially the same engine, heavily modified), cart-size anxiety driving the Ganon's Castle fallback, 100+ tester Mario Club feedback, and a late rebuild of the Water Temple.

### Cited Findings
- Wikipedia: OoT "originally ran on the same engine as Super Mario 64 but was so heavily modified that Miyamoto considers the final products different engines"; cartridge target grew from 16 to 32 MB; the Ganon's-Castle-as-hub worst case with painting-like portals, and the Forest Temple's Phantom Ganon painting boss as an idea from that stage. — [Wikipedia: OoT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Legend_of_Zelda:_Ocarina_of_Time) (cites other sources; developer origin of the painting claim not independently verified).
- Miyamoto: "Zelda was about half finished when we were making Mario." Tezuka: Mario used "about 60%" of N64 capability, Zelda "80 or even 90". Children testers "can enjoy games just by playing around"; unclear if it was interesting. — [1101 "Half Done by Mario"](https://glitterberri.com/ocarina-of-time/1101-interviews/half-done-by-mario/)
- Iwawaki: after Super Mario Club testers were hired "everything was suddenly changed at 30% completion"; more than 100 people supported testing. — [1101 "Tough to Fix (Part 2)"](https://glitterberri.com/ocarina-of-time/1101-interviews/tough-to-fix-part-2/)
- Miyamoto said he moved from heavy sword combat to a simpler system so the game was easier than Mario 64 (combat scope, not covered here). — [shmuplations](https://shmuplations.com/ocarinaoftime/)
- Aonuma: "I wanted to do something about the Water Temple. Everyone talks about it, so it's been a longstanding wish of mine these 13 years!... The worst thing was thinking about how many people may have given up there." Iwata: "like a bone caught in your throat". — [Iwata Asks Vol. 4, p3](https://www.nintendo.com/en-gb/Iwata-Asks/Iwata-Asks-The-Legend-of-Zelda-Ocarina-of-Time-3D/Vol-4-Development-Staff/3-I-ve-Got-to-Fix-the-Water-Temple-/3-I-ve-Got-to-Fix-the-Water-Temple--235888.html)
- Aonuma GDC 2004: gamedeveloper.com notes he gave a speech on "The Miyamoto way" and that he realised after Wind Waker Zelda had no "really new ideas" since OoT (2007 talk). Dungeon-theming passage and transcript not read. — [Game Developer: Post-GDC](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/game-platforms/post-gdc-aonuma-s-reflections-on-i-zelda-i-)
- 2026 (Switch 2 remake): Wikipedia says a Switch 2 remake released 5 Nov 2026. Game Developer (2 Nov 2026) reports Aonuma, "who worked on the original as the dungeon designer and game planner", saying he wanted the current team to learn "the game structure and development style" and that recent games moved from "visiting dungeons in a linear path and collecting principal items" to open-air exploration. These are 2026 remake-era sources, not 1998 evidence; the earlier "Switch 2" search hits are now corroborated by Wikipedia but their specific claims (warp points etc.) remain unread. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Legend_of_Zelda:_Ocarina_of_Time), [Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/production/zelda-producer-ocarina-of-time-remake-helps-junior-staff-learn-classic-insights)

### Inferences
- Aonuma's later complaint about the linear "dungeon, item, dungeon" formula frames OoT as the origin of the structure BotW later dismantled.

### Gaps
- Cartridge-space cuts in dungeons, Ura Zelda/Master Quest contents (Wikipedia notes Ura Zelda held cut content) not detailed; GDC 2004 transcript unread.

## 6. Analyst models (Mark Brown, Boss Keys) and comparison to other Zeldas

### Takeaway
I could only verify Boss Keys' general method, not the OoT episode.

### Cited Findings
- Game Developer on Boss Keys: it breaks Zelda dungeons down "through a simple analysis of keys, doors, and special items"; diagrams drop physical layout for the order of obstacles encountered, shown for Oracle of Ages' Jabu Jabu's Belly with water levels governing access. Brown says that layout makes it "far easier for the player to navigate than Ocarina of Time's infamous Water Temple", though the Water Temple benefits from 3D "because it lets the player visually understand the concept of floating platforms". Oracle dungeons "set up a clear and functional pattern of exploration" that Brown argues beat some OoT dungeons. — [Game Developer: Boss Keys write-up](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/take-a-close-look-at-the-level-design-of-some-exceptional-i-zelda-i-games)
- "Find the path / follow the path": not found in any readable source. UNVERIFIED as a Boss Keys term.
- Comparison to BotW: Aonuma frames the move from linear dungeon sequence to open-air exploration (see section 5).

### Inferences
- Key/door/item graphs plus a "what state is the world in" annotation (water level) are the lens the Water Temple fits worst, which matches Brown's comment above.

### Gaps
- Boss Keys OoT/MM/TP/BotW graphs and quotations. Needs amara.org or YouTube captions.

## 7. Coherence: central landmark and state changes

### Takeaway
Developers confirm the Forest Temple's central twisting hall and the Water Temple's repeated trips; critics describe the hub-and-state-change pattern.

### Cited Findings
- Onozuka: Forest Temple has the twisting path "at the center"; reaching previously unreachable items by using it. Water Temple requires travelling "countless times to and from an area". — [1101 "Dungeons (Part 2)"](https://glitterberri.com/ocarina-of-time/1101-interviews/dungeons-part-2/)
- Deku Tree: whole dungeon around "one large, open central area". — [Game Developer Part 1](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/audio/ocarina-of-time-dungeons-ranked-part-1)
- Critic claims about Forest Temple central-room torches lighting after each Poe, and Water Temple "tower hub with three levels" come from Zelda Dungeon articles known only through search summaries. UNVERIFIED. — [Forest Temple](https://www.zeldadungeon.net/?p=10887), [Water Temple](https://www.zeldadungeon.net/?p=11084)

### Inferences
- A single readable central space reused under changed conditions (twisted hall, water level) is what makes both dungeons feel like one place; Fire Temple's landmark-less rooms are the cited counterexample.

### Gaps
- No direct developer discussion of "central landmark" as a principle.
