## Why does Link jump automatically at ledges? (developer accounts)

### Takeaway
Per the Iwata Asks (2011) material, the team originally had a jump button; Miyamoto came up with auto-jump (his account: on a day off, announced the following Monday), and says it let the team control Link's pose and animation per jump. Aonuma stressed it let designers restrict where players could jump. Accounts of who invented it and why are reported as inconsistent. All of this is unverified against full text.

### Cited Findings
- Koizumi said a button press was needed to jump when development of Ocarina began (the early build had no auto-jump; a jump button was later dropped). Relayed via search summary (quote reported: "you had to press a button to jump when we first started making The Legend of Zelda: Ocarina of Time") — [Iwata Asks Vol.2 Part 1, "Chanbara-style Action"](https://nintendo.co.uk/Iwata-Asks/Iwata-Asks-The-Legend-of-Zelda-Ocarina-of-Time-3D/Vol-2-Original-Development-Staff-Part-1/2-The-Legend-of-Zelda-with-Chanbara-style-Action/2-The-Legend-of-Zelda-with-Chanbara-style-Action-231626.html) (not fetched; snippet only)
- Miyamoto, in his Iwata Asks session, says he thought of auto-jump on a day off and announced it to staff on Monday, and the staff were stunned (summary paraphrase) — [Iwata Asks Vol.5, Miyamoto](https://nintendo.co.uk/Iwata-Asks/Iwata-Asks-The-Legend-of-Zelda-Ocarina-of-Time-3D/Vol-5-Mr-Shigeru-Miyamoto/5-A-Sword-Sorcery-Tale-Admired-Worldwide/5-A-Sword-Sorcery-Tale-Admired-Worldwide-224778.html); fan-site summary: [Zelda Dungeon](https://www.zeldadungeon.net/?p=2593)
- Miyamoto: auto-jump let the team program the pose Link takes after jumping, because the game can look at terrain ahead and decide how Link jumps (e.g. the headfirst dive off a waterfall ledge) — same Vol.5 page, via search summary.
- Aonuma called auto-jump "convenient" from a development standpoint: there were places where they preferred players not to jump, so auto-jump gave more control over where players could go (paraphrase via search summary) — [Nintendo World Report](https://nintendoworldreport.com/news/26926) and Iwata Asks pages; not fetched.
- Earlier framing: a Famimaga 64 interview of 26 Nov 1997 reportedly has Miyamoto describing "full auto jump" as a deliberate experiment because Nintendo had already made a jumping game — [Zelda Dungeon wiki, Famimaga 64 Nov 26 1997](https://www.zeldadungeon.net/wiki/Interview:Famimaga_64_November_26th_1997) (snippet only; confirm wording)
- Search summaries note Aonuma's and Miyamoto's accounts of who conceived it and why conflict — [Zeldawiki Auto Jump](https://zeldawiki.wiki/wiki/Auto_Jump) (not fetched; reliability of this claim unverified)
- Iwata Asks (2011) staff also recall auto-jump and Z-targeting impressing players new to 3D (paraphrase) — [Iwata Asks Vol.2 pt1 page 0/4](https://iwataasks.nintendo.com/interviews/3ds/zelda-ocarina-of-time/0/4)
- Remake comparison: search results report that a later remake added a jump button, and an article titled "Nintendo explains why Zelda: Ocarina of Time remake's auto jump was hard to drop" exists. The results mention a "Switch 2 remake" with 2026 dates; I could not verify this, and it conflicts with the brief's 3DS remake framing. Treat as unverified. Note also that Nintendo's 3DS remake discussion of removing auto-jump is at [Inven Global](https://www.invenglobal.com/articles/28139/the-legend-of-zelda-ocarina-of-time-remake-changing-the-visuals-preserving-the-experience) and [VGC](https://videogameschronicle.com/news/nintendo-explains-why-zelda-ocarina-of-time-remakes-auto-jump-was-hard-to-drop) (neither fetched).

### Inferences
- Auto-jump trades player agency for authored control: designers can guarantee safe, readable gaps and animate each jump, which a manual jump cannot. For a team with manual jump, double jump, wall kicks and ledge grabs, this shows what is given up (precision, expressiveness) and bought (level-design control, animation quality, single-button simplicity).

## Camera (and comparison with Mario 64)

### Takeaway
The team tied Z-targeting to camera/targeting problems in Mario 64 (circling signs). Koizumi said a first-person view would have made camera and battle handling much easier, but they wanted Link visible throughout. Details of the automatic camera, fixed cameras, and first-person aim were not obtained.

### Cited Findings
- Koizumi (reported quote): thanks to keeping Link visible the whole time "it got really hard in ways that would have been extremely easy in first-person, like how to handle the camera and battles" — [Iwata Asks Vol.2 Part 1](https://iwataasks.nintendo.com/interviews/3ds/zelda-ocarina-of-time/1/2) (snippet)
- Osawa: in Mario 64, trying to read a sign you would sometimes go around it in circles; Koizumi: hard to line Mario's facing up with targets such as a sign — [Iwata Asks pages 1/2-1/3](https://iwataasks.nintendo.com/interviews/3ds/zelda-ocarina-of-time/1/3); also [Siliconera](https://www.siliconera.com/z-targeting-in-the-legend-of-zelda-ocarina-of-time-was-inspired-by-ninjas/)
- Koizumi: discussion of hitting the enemy in front began during Mario 64 development; a stage sword fight at Toei Kyoto Studio Park gave the clue — same sources.

### Inferences
- The early plan for a first-person view (in the brief) is partly corroborated by Koizumi's remark, but was not confirmed.

### Gaps
- Camera modes, fixed cameras, Lakitu-vs-OoT comparison, team-described camera problems, early first-person plan details.
