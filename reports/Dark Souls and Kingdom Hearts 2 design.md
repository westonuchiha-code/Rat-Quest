# Keep the Souls rules, borrow Kingdom Hearts' speed

Dark Souls is fair because it is readable, not because it is slow. Its enemies show each blow before it lands, commit to it, and leave an opening afterwards. Its levels warn you about traps and reward you for noticing. Its world folds back on itself through shortcuts, so what you just fought through turns into a quick road home. None of that needs slow animations. FromSoftware proved it themselves: Bloodborne and Sekiro sped the formula up, dropped the shield, put the hero in the air, and kept every one of those rules. Kingdom Hearts 2 gets its speed another way. The hero starts modest and stacks growth on growth: Drive Forms that run and jump up to **1.7 times** as fast, a glide that ends at **4 times** running speed, longer combos, and dodges whose safe moment grows from **0.3 to 1.1 seconds**. Its best late fights then rein that speed in with a hidden, fixed budget: after so many hits the boss breaks out and strikes back. For RATQUEST the conclusion is that the game is already closer to this blend than the owner may think. The knight is fast (his swing is live in 0.14 seconds, faster than any enemy's warning), the enemies follow Souls rules (warnings, commitment, openings, and ways of refusing to be mashed), and the camera has been rebuilt round the problems both games are known for. What is missing is mostly at the world and progression level. Wall kicks should become the shortcuts that fold each world back on itself. The knight's speed should grow across the four worlds the way Sora's does, rather than being complete in the first room. And the enemies' anti-mashing budgets, which the game already has, should be made visible.

The closest things to primary sources are two translations. The first is the Fextralife translation of the Famitsu interview printed in the *Dark Souls Design Works* book, in which Miyazaki and his artists talk area by area ([Design Works](https://darksouls.wiki.fextralife.com/Dark+Souls+1+-+Design+Works+Interview)). The second is a fan translation of Square Enix's official *Kingdom Hearts II Ultimania* guide, kept on the KH Wiki forum as "Notes 53" ([Notes 53](https://www.khwiki.com/Forum:Notes_53_(KHII,_mechanics_%2B_backups_from_Pastebin)_-_Ultima_Spark)). Below those come the community wikis (Fextralife for Dark Souls frame tables and boss patterns, khwiki.com for Kingdom Hearts 2), which are careful but unofficial. Below those come analysts' opinions: Celia Wagar's CritPoints essays and Robert Boyd's Game Developer article. Then Wikipedia, which paraphrases developer interviews nobody here opened. What could not be read matters, because it is where most of the developers' own words live. ## Dark Souls builds one folded world from rough maps

**Lordran is "a single interconnected world divided into distinct regions and linked by unlockable shortcuts"**, in which areas can often be reached by several routes and some paths stay locked until conditions are met ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dark_Souls_(video_game))). The developers' account of how they built it is the most solid material in the notes, because it comes from the Design Works interview. **Miyazaki says the map came before everything else**: "The map design was really what dictated everything else, once we determined what needed to happen in each area we would immediately draw up a rough map, then once the basic layout of the area was decided, we'd work on the finer details." The rough map was how he told the artists what an area needed, how it fitted together and what it looked like, and he says "this is actually how most of the areas were constructed" ([Design Works](https://darksouls.wiki.fextralife.com/Dark+Souls+1+-+Design+Works+Interview)). Some areas, the Great Hollow and Ash Lake, were made "directly from the rough map" with almost no concept art. Blighttown was built from its largest feature outwards: "we began with large features like the water wheel elevator" and built the area up from there.

**The hub was designed first and built to connect everywhere.** Firelink Shrine was "one of the first places we designed"; it "is a small area but it connects to many different places and has many hidden spots". One artist says it "was made to connect with areas in every direction. In fact, we had to remove some routes in the final game" ([Design Works](https://darksouls.wiki.fextralife.com/Dark+Souls+1+-+Design+Works+Interview)). The community wiki traces the folds that make this work. An elevator from Firelink comes up in the Undead Parish once it is unlocked, so a place you fought your way to becomes a short ride from home. A cave in the Valley of Drakes "is actually a shortcut" into Blighttown that skips two whole areas. A circular elevator in the Demon Ruins returns you to Quelaag's Domain ([Undead Parish](https://darksouls.wiki.fextralife.com/Undead+Parish), [Valley of Drakes](https://darksouls.wiki.fextralife.com/Valley+of+Drakes), [Demon Ruins](https://darksouls.wiki.fextralife.com/Demon+Ruins)). Wagar, an analyst, describes the result as "multithreaded": the world "folds back in on itself", its shortcuts "make runbacks shorter", and its levels "make use of the Z axis to thread levels back through themselves" ([CritPoints](https://critpoints.net/2015/03/19/what-makes-the-souls-series-so-appealing-to-you-compare-to-other-action-rpgs/)).

**The openness is partly an illusion, and that is deliberate.** Boyd argues that Lordran "appears to be very non-linear at first glance, but in actuality it's a lot more linear than it seems". There are three phases: ring two bells; pass Sen's Fortress, which "acts as an exam"; then the whole world opens. Even at the first fork, "Dark Souls is subtly funneling the character into the course of least resistance", because the enemies on the right road look like the tutorial's ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/deep-dungeon-exploring-the-design-of-i-dark-souls-i-)). That is opinion, but it fits the developers' own words about pacing. Miyazaki says that after the bells and the traps of Sen's Fortress, "I really wanted [the] player to feel 'Yes! I've made it!'" on reaching Anor Londo, and that they "spent a great deal of time" on it because it is the midpoint ([Design Works](https://darksouls.wiki.fextralife.com/Dark+Souls+1+-+Design+Works+Interview)).

**Bonfires make the loops matter.** A bonfire restores health and healing flasks and brings back most enemies other than bosses, and death sends you back to the last one you rested at ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dark_Souls_(video_game))). Every shortcut therefore shortens the walk back after a death, which is why opening one feels like a reward. Boyd adds that refilling flasks at every bonfire means "the player is encouraged to use all of the resources at their disposal" instead of hoarding ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/deep-dungeon-exploring-the-design-of-i-dark-souls-i-)). **The Lordvessel, given after the midpoint boss, lets you warp between bonfires** ([Fextralife](https://darksouls.wiki.fextralife.com/Lordvessel)). Many players think the game's second half is weaker, with Lost Izalith the usual target. That view comes only from forum opinion, and others call the complaint overblown ([ResetEra](https://www.resetera.com/threads/the-dark-souls-has-a-bad-second-half-meme-is-overblown-imo.61495/page-2)). No developer statement that the late areas were rushed was found, and the popular "they ran out of time" story is unverified. What Miyazaki does say is that he "would have liked to spend a little more time" on the Duke's Archives, and that the team has a "tendency to overproduce things and before you know it, the project can spiral out of control" ([Design Works](https://darksouls.wiki.fextralife.com/Dark+Souls+1+-+Design+Works+Interview)). A reasonable reading, though it is only a reading: once warping removes the need to walk the loops, the late areas lose the thing that made the early ones sing.

**Areas teach by showing, not telling.** For Sen's Fortress the artists "almost tried hard to make [the traps] obvious and create things that screamed 'trap!'", and Miyazaki calls it "almost comical how obvious they are". The worn steps where boulders roll are a warning he doubts first-time players notice: "Perhaps the second time, it will serve to remind them." Anor Londo was meant as "an area with no clear road, to have the player walk in places that you wouldn't normally walk, such as the buttresses". And the tutorial was drawn last, because "it's much easier to design it once you know what needs to be communicated" ([Design Works](https://darksouls.wiki.fextralife.com/Dark+Souls+1+-+Design+Works+Interview)). Boyd's favourite example is a boar in the Undead Parish. It is too strong to fight head on, but there are stairs to escape up, items on a ledge above it to lure you, and a fire nearby. "By not explicitly telling the player what to do but by leading them towards the answer, Dark Souls allows the player to feel clever" ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/deep-dungeon-exploring-the-design-of-i-dark-souls-i-)).

## Dark Souls fights are fair because they are readable, not because they are slow

The numbers in Dark Souls describe risk for every action, not slowness. **Attacking, blocking, running, jumping, rolling and parrying all cost stamina**, which comes back at 45 a second. Swinging stops it coming back until the swing ends, and a block that empties the bar breaks your guard and stuns you ([Fextralife: Stamina](https://darksouls.wiki.fextralife.com/Stamina)). **The roll's safe moment is the same 11 frames at every weight** (13 for the lightest "ninja flip"). What weight changes is the tail. A light roll lasts 21 to 24 frames in all. A heavy "fat roll" lasts 46 to 48, and ends with 25 frames in which you take 1.4 times the damage ([Fextralife: Equip Load](https://darksouls.wiki.fextralife.com/Equip+Load)). That is community data, and it disagrees with figures once seen on the Fandom wiki, so treat it as fairly but not fully sure. The wiki does not say what rate the frames are counted at. At the console game's 30 a second (my conversion), 11 frames is about 0.37 seconds of safety. The lesson is in the shape, not the speed: the dodge wins for a short, fixed time, and the punishment lives in the recovery afterwards. A faster game keeps that shape by shortening both together.

**Every boss described on the wiki follows the same grammar: few moves, each with a visible wind-up, and a recovery you can punish.** The Taurus Demon's standing smash "has a long, obvious buildup", and the answer is to circle behind him. The Capra Demon's two-handed swing breaks guard but is one of the easiest to see coming, because "he takes the time to put his weapons together then wind up". Smough's hammer "is slow and has poor tracking", with a slow recovery that is the opening, and the pillars split him from Ornstein ([Taurus](https://darksouls.wiki.fextralife.com/Taurus+Demon), [Capra](https://darksouls.wiki.fextralife.com/Capra+Demon), [Ornstein and Smough](https://darksouls.wiki.fextralife.com/Ornstein+and+Smough)). **Difficulty climbs by adding things around the moves, not by hiding them.** Capra's dogs "can easily stun-lock you", so the fight becomes about killing them first on the stairs, and the arena's pillars shape the double fight. **Tracking is the dial the designers turn.** The Asylum Demon's hammer tracks "somewhat so beware of dodging too early". Artorias, a later and much faster boss, has "considerable range and tracking, punishing all but the most perfectly timed" dodges, and the advice is to hit "once or twice", not greedily ([Asylum Demon](https://darksouls.wiki.fextralife.com/Asylum+Demon), [Artorias](https://darksouls.wiki.fextralife.com/Artorias+the+Abysswalker)). The idea that the early bosses form a deliberate lesson plan is my inference; the wiki does not say so.

Analysts name four principles, all opinion, all from CritPoints. **Commitment**: an action you cannot cancel feels weighty, and being able to dodge out of anything at any time "reduces the feeling of commitment" ([CritPoints](https://critpoints.net/2015/03/24/treatise-on-commitment-in-action-games/)). **Matched speed**: "the standard attack speed of most weapons is roughly the same speed as enemy attacks", so swinging after an enemy starts usually means it hits first, and the fight becomes positioning and punishing misses ([CritPoints](https://critpoints.net/2020/09/12/how-dark-souls-changed-combat/)). **Spacing**: the character cannot turn while swinging, so stepping round a swinging enemy is the core move, and with almost no jumping the fights are "mostly 2d" ([CritPoints](https://critpoints.net/2015/03/20/65/)). **Fair crowds**: group fights stay fair because enemies "are committed to their own attacks" ([CritPoints](https://critpoints.net/2017/03/24/multiple-enemy-fights-are-fair/)).

Miyazaki's own stated aim survives only in paraphrase. Wikipedia reports that the team meant to keep and raise the challenge "not simply by making the game harder", but by giving players freedom to experiment and feel rewarded ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dark_Souls_(video_game))). His 2024 remark that players should understand why they died, and feel the death was justified, is known only from a search summary of an unreadable page ([Game Informer, unverified](https://gameinformer.com/interview/2024/06/24/from-softwares-hidetaka-miyazaki-discusses-his-approach-to-difficulty)). The weak points critics name are the camera and the lock-on. The camera fails near walls, an essay notes that one game "slowly push[es] you into the wall" in group fights, and a reader argues that telling players to drop lock-on in groups "says more about the mechanic itself" ([CritPoints](https://critpoints.net/2017/03/24/multiple-enemy-fights-are-fair/)). Forum complaints about lock-on aiming at the middle of giant bosses are anecdotal.

| Dark Souls' APPROACH (what makes it fair) | Dark Souls' PACE (what makes it slow) |
|---|---|
| Every blow has a wind-up you can see | Long wind-ups and heavy recovery tails |
| Enemies commit, then are open | Player swings as slow as enemy swings |
| The dodge is safe for a short, fixed time; the tail is the risk | A stamina bar that stops you after a few actions |
| Few moves per boss; difficulty from terrain and company | Fights "mostly 2d", rooted to the ground |
| Deaths you can understand | Long runbacks before shortcuts open |

## FromSoftware's own faster games kept the rules and dropped the shield

This is the strongest evidence that the approach can be separated from the pace, because the same studio did it three times. **Bloodborne's team chose not to raise the difficulty**, which would have made it "pretty much unplayable for anyone". Instead they made "a more aggressive combat system focusing on both action and strategy". Guns replaced shields. A gunshot timed against an attack opens the enemy to a heavy counter. And "rally" lets you win back lost health by striking back quickly ([Wikipedia: Bloodborne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bloodborne)). Miyazaki's reported reason, that shields made Demon's Souls feel "a bit passive", comes only from search summaries ([NeoGAF translation, unverified](https://www.neogaf.com/threads/bloodborne-4gamer-interview-with-hidetaka-miyazaki-fully-translated.840059/)). **Sekiro went further.** Fights revolve around breaking the enemy's posture and deflecting its blows, "eventually leading to an opening" for one killing strike. It added a jump button and a grappling hook, where earlier games felt "rooted firmly to the ground" ([Wikipedia: Sekiro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sekiro:_Shadows_Die_Twice)). Miyazaki reportedly called Sekiro a "turning point" and said the studio's games could get faster still, but that too is a search summary only ([GamesRadar, unverified](https://gamesradar.com/games/action/sekiro-was-a-turning-point-for-fromsoftware-says-hidetaka-miyazaki-who-thinks-the-studios-games-can-still-get-faster-theres-one-more-level-we-can-crank-it-up-to)). **Elden Ring** returned to shields but kept Sekiro-like combat ([Wikipedia: Elden Ring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elden_Ring)).

Across all three, what stayed is plain. Enemy attacks are readable and leave time to answer. Some meter limits how long you can stay safe or passive (stamina, then rally, then posture). Bosses use one move at a time. What changed is how long the player's own actions take, what "defence" means (a held block, then a timed gunshot, then a timed deflect), and whether the hero leaves the ground. No single Miyazaki statement lists what was kept; that list is my reading of the three Wikipedia summaries. The direction is clear all the same. **Going faster at FromSoftware meant turning defence from holding a button into timing one, and giving the player vertical movement, while the enemy side of the contract stayed the same.**

## Kingdom Hearts 2 gets fast by stacking growth, then checks it with a hidden budget

### Combos, gap-closers and auto-aim make it fluid

Kingdom Hearts 2's combat is a chain of hits that ends in a finisher. Abilities buy extra combo length: Combo Plus adds one hit on the ground, Air Combo Plus one in the air ([KH Wiki: Abilities](https://www.khwiki.com/Growth_Ability)). Wikipedia reports, in paraphrase, that the battle director Yuichi Kanemori "drew inspiration from fighting games" because "players feel exhilaration when executing fighting maneuvers". It also reports that a programmer built a tool so Kanemori could fine-tune actions and their numbers quickly ([Wikipedia: KH2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_Hearts_II)). **Much of the feeling of flow comes from moves that close distance for you.** Slide Dash "instantly closes in on and attacks a far target", and Aerial Spiral "closes in on far targets in midair". Without a lock-on, Sora "will automatically attack the closest available target" ([KH Wiki: Abilities](https://www.khwiki.com/Growth_Ability), [KH Wiki: Lock-On](https://www.khwiki.com/Lock-On)). How far "far" is, and how fast these moves travel, is not in any page read. The commitment sits at the end of the chain, not in each hit: after the finisher "a cool-down period happens for Sora before he can start a new set of attacks" ([KH Wiki forum: Notes 41](https://www.khwiki.com/Forum:Notes_41_(Revenge_Value))). The same team moved camera control from the shoulder buttons to the right stick after complaints about the first game ([Wikipedia: KH2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_Hearts_II)).

### The speed grows in three stacked layers

The official guide's numbers, via the fan translation, show exactly how Sora gets faster ([Notes 53](https://www.khwiki.com/Forum:Notes_53_(KHII,_mechanics_%2B_backups_from_Pastebin)_-_Ultima_Spark)). **The first layer is the Drive Forms**, earned in story order, each a temporary transformation. **The second is the growth abilities**, movement moves that level up from 1 to MAX as each form is levelled, and then carry over into Sora's normal form at reduced strength (High Jump at about 70%). **The third is combo length and defence**, both of which also grow. Each form levels by doing a different thing ([KH Wiki: Drive Form](https://khwiki.com/Drive_Form)). Valor levels by landing hits, Wisdom by defeating Heartless, Master by picking up drive orbs, Final by defeating Nobodies. So each form rewards a different style of play.

| What grows | Early | Late | Source grade |
|---|---|---|---|
| Run and jump speed in a form | Master 1.25x, Valor and Wisdom 1.4x | Final Form 1.7x | Ultimania (fan translation) |
| Glide top speed (vs running) | 2x at level 1 | 4x at MAX, building up gradually | Ultimania (fan translation) |
| High Jump (height x distance) | 1.3 x 1.7 | 3.2 x 2.4 | Ultimania (fan translation) |
| Quick Run distance | 1.5 | 3.7 | Ultimania (fan translation) |
| Aerial Dodge (a second jump) | 0.8 high, 1.7 far | 1.6 high, 2.5 far | Ultimania (fan translation) |
| Dodge Roll safe time (Final Mix) | 0.3 s | 1.1 s (distance does not grow) | Ultimania (fan translation) |
| Quick Run safe time at the start | none | 0.5 s | Ultimania (fan translation) |
| Combo length | base | +1 Valor, +2 air in Master, more with abilities | KH Wiki and Ultimania |

The growth is earned, and it is a big part of why the late game feels so different from the early one. The Ultimania numbers are close to official, though the claim that this growth is *why* the late game feels fast is my inference. The safe moments of the defensive moves grow alongside the speed, so the game expects late bosses to be faster. Exact base speeds, attack frame data and how much faster forms really swing are not in the notes.

### The best fights are budgets the player learns to spend

Fast combos would let a player hit a boss until it dies, and Kingdom Hearts 2 stops that with what fans call **Revenge Value**. Every attack adds a hidden number. Every boss has a hidden limit. When the limit is reached the boss "can and will" break out of the combo with a set counter-move "even if it's still staggered" ([Notes 41](https://www.khwiki.com/Forum:Notes_41_(Revenge_Value))). The official guide confirms the mechanism exists: most bosses, taking continuous damage, recover and counterattack partway ([Notes 53](https://www.khwiki.com/Forum:Notes_53_(KHII,_mechanics_%2B_backups_from_Pastebin)_-_Ultima_Spark)). The community's numbers put a basic ground hit at 1, a ground finisher at 3 and an aerial finisher at 3.5. Typical bosses sit at 12.5 and the secret Lingering Will at 25 ([Notes 41](https://www.khwiki.com/Forum:Notes_41_(Revenge_Value)), [Notes 53](https://www.khwiki.com/Forum:Notes_53_(KHII,_mechanics_%2B_backups_from_Pastebin)_-_Ultima_Spark)). Those are fan-collected figures, and the notes warn that some were attached to the wrong boss when extracted.

The two fights the wiki treats as the game's peaks are both about managing that budget. Against Roxas, "Revenge Value is the main danger". The advice is to stop after one finisher so he keeps looping attacks you know. His unblockable Keyblade Release must be dodged, and guarding the last dash of one combo staggers him for "multiple seconds". In his harder rematch he "can now retaliate after just one finisher" ([KH Wiki: Roxas](https://www.khwiki.com/Game:Roxas)). Against the Lingering Will, the advice is to "back off before dealing a 5th normal combo finisher in a row". His rush is three air dashes whose "timings ... are easy to predict", and one attack is "telegraphed by a distinct sound cue" ([KH Wiki: Lingering Will](https://www.khwiki.com/Game:Lingering_Will)). **This is Dark Souls' "hit once or twice, not greedily" rule, reached from the other end: a fast game that lets you be greedy, then charges for it on a fixed, learnable schedule.** Wagar's criticism is the important caveat: the system is "totally opaque. There's no feedback telling you how much revenge value you're dealing ... it's a matter of memorization", and she links it to Dark Souls' poise ([CritPoints](https://critpoints.net/2018/02/19/kingdom-hearts-2-revenge-value)).

Two more points complete the picture. Critical Mode, the hardest setting, keeps every enemy's behaviour the same but doubles the damage you take and caps your health growth at 60 ([KH Wiki: Difficulty](https://www.khwiki.com/Difficulty_Level)). So the same fast fights become a one-mistake game. And the game is easy to mash on its lower settings: the guide itself says of one enemy, "You can easily dispatch it by pressing X and Triangle repeatedly" ([Notes 53](https://www.khwiki.com/Forum:Notes_53_(KHII,_mechanics_%2B_backups_from_Pastebin)_-_Ultima_Spark)). Its context-sensitive Reaction Commands rarely punish an early press: "Only three Reaction Commands in the entirety of Kingdom Hearts II penalize such an action" ([KH Wiki: Reaction Command](https://www.khwiki.com/Reaction_Command)). Reviewers praised the "divine" combo potential and the confident controls, and complained of "forced and awkward camera positions" ([Wikipedia: KH2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_Hearts_II)). Complaints that it feels floaty or is mashable could not be found in any source that could be read.

## What survives when the pace goes up

Put the three bodies of evidence together and a short list survives every increase in speed. **Readable warnings survive** in Dark Souls' wind-ups, in Bloodborne and Sekiro, and in the Lingering Will's sound cue. **Commitment survives**, but it moves. In Dark Souls it lives in every swing and in the roll's recovery. In Kingdom Hearts 2 it lives at the end of the chain (the finisher's cool-down) and in the boss's break-out. **A limit on staying safe or greedy survives**: stamina, rally, posture, revenge value. **Short, fixed safe moments in dodges survive**, though Kingdom Hearts 2 lets them grow with progress. **Terrain and company as the difficulty ramp survive**, from Capra's stairs to Roxas's arena. What does not survive is the slowness itself: matched slow swings, a stamina bar that stops you after a few actions, and fights rooted to the floor. FromSoftware dropped two of those three on its own. The one real tension is the one Wagar names. A fast game that relies on hidden budgets becomes a memory test, so the faster the hero, the more the enemy's limits need to be **seen**.

## What this means for RATQUEST

### Where the game already stands

The repository shows a game that has already taken the Souls approach without the Souls pace. The knight runs at 6.2 units a second, about 5.6 of his own heights. His first swing is live after 0.14 seconds and lasts 0.46 seconds in all, and the third hit takes 0.78 seconds, steps further and does double damage (`src/player/animations.ts`). Every enemy's warning is longer than that: the roach's quick bite takes 0.4 seconds, the mantis's cut 0.5, the Keep Guard's thrust 0.6, the Stag Knight's slam 0.9 (`src/data/tuning.ts`, HANDOFF). So the knight is faster than his enemies, the opposite of Dark Souls' matched speeds, and in that respect the game is already on Kingdom Hearts 2's side. Fairness is kept the Souls way: by commitment, and by enemies that refuse to be mashed. A dodge can cut off the end of a swing but not the swing itself. The roll lasts 0.5 seconds with about 0.33 seconds in which hits pass through, the backstep 0.3 seconds, and there is a 0.5 second wait before the next one. The perfect guard window is 0.16 seconds, doubled by the Buckler. That roll is about the same safe moment as Dark Souls' light roll (my conversion of its frames) and Kingdom Hearts 2's first-level Dodge Roll. The enemies' refusals look a great deal like revenge value and poise. The Keep Guard stabs back at once after two blocked blows in 2.2 seconds. The mantis hops away when a swing starts, and after two hits in a row. The Ant Pikeman springs back 0.38 seconds after the first blow. The Stag Knight does not flinch, and kneels only after five blows. The Broodmother has poise 7, and Grimsqueak bursts away after 3 hits per opening. The owner's sense that the game handles the approach "pretty well" is borne out by the code. The suggestions below build on that rather than replace it, and none of them makes the game easier.

### 1. Make the wall-kick climbs the shortcuts that fold each world back on itself

Death sends the knight back to the start of the level he is in, at full health (`src/player/controller.ts`), and most of the Foundry, Hollows and Keep have not yet been reworked. So each of those three worlds should get at least one wall-kick climb (a chimney, or a wall-to-wall chain) whose top opens a one-way way back: a gate unbarred from the far side, a lift sent down, a plank kicked over a gap. The way back should lead towards the world's spawn or its boss door. In the Hollows that could be a chimney near the Brood Pit's door on the gallery's north ledge. In the Keep it could be a climb from the inner ward back over the gatehouse to the courtyard. The second time through, the hard climb becomes the quick road home, and that is the Dark Souls "aha". This answers the owner's request for "far more use of the wall jump" with climbs that matter twice, and Anor Londo's buttresses are the model for routes over walls "you wouldn't normally walk". **Rests on**: the Firelink and Lordran shortcut findings and the bonfire-runback link. **Sourcing**: good for the structure (Design Works translation and the Fextralife layout pages). The claim that shortcuts shorten runbacks is analyst opinion (Wagar) and a paraphrase (Wikipedia). The feeling of opening one has no developer quote at all. Each new climb must pass the same checks as the Cistern's: the route played with the real controller, and the key wall taken away to prove it is the only way.

### 2. Put each collectible where a route also connects something

The owner wants fewer things to pick up, each earned by movement. The Cistern already does this, with eight things where there were thirty-two. Dark Souls' lesson adds one more layer: the reward at the top of a climb should sit beside something that changes the level, like the lever in suggestion 1. A bottle cap at the top of a chimney is good; a cap beside the lever that drops the lift home is better, because the movement earns both. Boyd's boar in the Parish, with lure items on the ledge above, shows items used to lead the player to a solution. **Rests on**: Boyd's boar example and the shortcut findings. **Sourcing**: weak to moderate. It is one analyst's example, and no developer statement on items as lures was found. Treat it as a design habit to try, not a proven rule.

### 3. Plan each remaining world from a rough map, starting from its biggest feature

Before the Foundry, Hollows and Keep are reworked, draw each as a rough top-down and side-on map first. Start from the largest feature, as Blighttown started from its water wheel: the Crucible in the Foundry, the chasm and gallery in the Hollows, the courtyard and battlements in the Keep. Then lay the loops and the wall-kick shortcuts over that map, and only then the dressing. The Club already is a Firelink, a small room with a door in every direction. The obvious next step is one late shortcut from deep in a world back into the Club itself. **Rests on**: Miyazaki's map-first process and the Firelink quotes. **Sourcing**: the best in this report (the Design Works interview, though in a fan translation). The jump checker (`src/levels/reach.ts`) and the shortcut tests already give the "rough map" a way to be checked.

### 4. Turn the glint colours into one rule for every creature

The Stag Knight already uses Dark Souls' readability as a code. A white glint means a blow a shield will stop, a red glint means one it will not. The firefly rings the floor where fire will land, and the Sump Wyrm's eyes go red before its charge. Make this a rule across the whole game, the way Sen's Fortress "screamed 'trap!'": every heavy blow a shield cannot stop shows red, every blockable one white, and every blow that runs along the floor (the stomp, the scissor, the whirl, the tail lash) shows the same ring the Stag Knight's does. Check the Broodmother's pounce and thread drop, and Grimsqueak's attacks, against it. Each creature already has sounds of its own, so a distinct warning sound for each red blow, like the Lingering Will's, is the second half. The hero stays silent; this is the enemies' voice, not his. **Rests on**: the Design Works trap quotes, the boss wind-up grammar on the Fextralife pages, and the Lingering Will's sound cue. **Sourcing**: good for "make danger obvious" (developer quotes in translation). Community wiki for the boss patterns. Miyazaki's "understand why you died" is unverified.

### 5. Show the enemies' patience running out

The game's anti-mashing limits are good design, and by Wagar's critique of Kingdom Hearts 2 they are at risk of being invisible. Give each one a tell that builds as the budget is spent. The Keep Guard's shield arm could shake harder after the first blocked blow. The Stag Knight's shell could crack and glow a little more with each of the five blows before it kneels. The Broodmother could tremble with poise spent. Grimsqueak's chest shard could pulse brighter with each of his three hits per opening, so the player learns to step back before he bursts away. The numbers do not change, so the game is no easier; it is only more readable, which is the Dark Souls half of the brief applied to the Kingdom Hearts half. **Rests on**: the revenge value findings and the CritPoints opacity critique. **Sourcing**: the mechanism is well documented (KH Wiki forum, confirmed by the official guide in translation). The case for making it visible is one analyst's opinion.

### 6. Let the knight's speed grow across the four worlds, sideways, not upwards

Today the knight has every move from the first room: the double jump, the long jump, the wall kick and its chain, the ledge grab and the plunge. Gear only trades one thing for another (the Royal Scarf's wall kicks go 1.15 times higher at the cost of a heart). Kingdom Hearts 2's late-game flash comes from growth earned along the way, so hold some flow back and hand it out at the two bosses. For example, beating the Sump Wyrm could lengthen the wall-chain carry or let a jump pressed mid-chain go further. Beating the Broodmother could add a short dash in the air. The growth should be in **distance and flow, not height**: the owner's complaint that he "can just jump over stuff", and the Mario 64 comparison in `research_notes/README.md`, both say the knight already climbs too high for his size. Later worlds are then built to need the new reach, which keeps the "only one way" checks honest, and their enemies are tuned to match (suggestion 7). The game is not made easier. The standing rule "never auto-equip" means an earned move should be learned or shown, not slipped on silently. **Rests on**: the Drive Form and growth-ability tables. **Sourcing**: the numbers come from the official guide in a fan translation, so fairly strong. That this growth is *why* the late game feels fast is my inference. Because this changes how every later level is checked, it is a large decision for the owner, not a tweak.

### 7. Use tracking and break-out budgets as the late-game dial

Every creature in the game turns with the knight for between 50% and 85% of its wind-up and then commits: the ant 50%, the guard and stag 55%, the mantis and Ironback 60%, the bat and Grimsqueak's swoop 75%, the firefly 85% (`src/enemies/*.ts`). That is a narrow band. Dark Souls widens it on purpose: the Asylum Demon punishes dodging too early, Artorias punishes anything but a perfectly timed dodge. Kingdom Hearts 2's rematches lower the break-out limit (Roxas retaliates "after just one finisher"). Spread the band across the game. Cistern and Foundry creatures commit early, which rewards a quick dodge. Keep creatures track later and break out sooner, which demands a late, exact dodge. So the enemies' demands grow as the knight's speed grows. **Rests on**: the tracking notes on the Asylum Demon and Artorias, and Roxas's rematch. **Sourcing**: community wikis. The "dial" framing is inference.

### 8. Give the air a committed sword stroke, or decide firmly not to

The knight's only attack in the air is the plunge (0.16 seconds held, a dive at 26, then 0.5 seconds stuck in the ground). Kingdom Hearts 2's flash is largely in the air, and Sekiro shows FromSoftware moving off the ground too. Two new flyers sit in the air on purpose: the Firefly Bomber hangs "inside a double jump", and the Hornet Lancer dives from above. A single, committed air slash would be the Kingdom Hearts 2 move most in keeping with the owner's "flashing movement". It should be one hit, spend the air jump, end a wall chain, and leave him falling with no second swing. Test it on a slider. It must not make the firefly or hornet easier, so their openings would need retuning if it ships. **Rests on**: the Kingdom Hearts 2 air-combo findings and Sekiro's vertical movement. **Sourcing**: the fact that Kingdom Hearts 2 has air combos is solid (wiki). That an air stroke suits this game is entirely inference. Of all the suggestions, this one carries the most risk to the Mario 64 feel.

### 9. Shape boss rooms with terrain, not extra creatures

Dark Souls raises a boss fight's difficulty with terrain and company: Capra's stairs and dogs, the pillars between Ornstein and Smough. Company is out, since the owner has ruled out wave fights. Terrain is open. The Sump Wyrm charges until it meets a wall and stuns itself, so a pillar or two in the Sump would let the player choose where the charge ends, like Smough's trample. The Broodmother's thread drop would gain from a ledge or two that change where she can follow. Grimsqueak's rings of light already run along the floor, so a raised step would turn jumping them into a choice of footing. Any change to the Sump must pass the twenty-fight stress run in `tests/wyrm.ts`, because the body that cannot tie itself up is the most fragile thing in that fight. **Rests on**: the Capra and Ornstein and Smough boss pages. **Sourcing**: community wiki strategy text, with no developer statement on intent.

### 10. Give the last world the most time, and the tutorial the last look

The common verdict on Dark Souls is that its late areas lost the hand-made connections of the early ones. The developers did not confirm rushing, but Miyazaki admits the urge to overproduce and an area he wished had more time. The Keep is the knight's Lost Izalith in waiting. Plan it with loops and wall-kick returns like the others, and do not let the pause menu's "warp out" make it a set of corridors. And as Miyazaki drew the Undead Asylum last, look again at the Cistern's first stretch once the other three worlds are done, so it teaches exactly what the later worlds ask. **Rests on**: the second-half opinions, Miyazaki's scope remarks and the tutorial-last quote. **Sourcing**: the tutorial point is a developer quote (in translation). The second-half verdict is forum opinion only, and the cause is unverified.

### What to leave behind

Several things in both games pull against the owner's wishes and should be named as rejected. A Dark Souls stamina bar would bring the slowness back. The game's commitment already does that job, and the stamina finding itself says the bar is about pacing, not fairness. Kingdom Hearts 2's safety nets (Once More and Second Chance, which hold Sora at 1 health), its forgiving Reaction Commands, and Final Form's automatic attacks all make fights easier, which the owner has ruled out. Bloodborne's rally gives back health for attacking; it is a fine idea for a slower game, but here it would soften fights that are already fast. Dark Souls' plain lock-on, criticised in its own analyst's comment thread, needs nothing taken from it: the knight's swings already turn to face a foe within reach, and a lock-on press with nobody near brings the view round behind him, which is the Souls habit. The camera rework (right stick only, rising over low things, lifting over his head against walls) already answers the two weaknesses both games are known for. What remains there is to test it with the Stag Knight and the Broodmother backed against walls, where Dark Souls' camera is weakest.
